# Amplifier & Connector Layer

Humanity Loop should not stop at finding, auditing, and reporting problems.

When a Scout finds a **verified promising project, researcher, invention, intervention, or public-interest idea**, the system should ask:

> What does this need next, and who can provide it?

## Core pipeline

Scout
→ Verifier
→ Needs Assessor
→ Resource Matcher
→ Connector
→ Amplifier
→ Outcome Tracker

## Needs Assessor

Classify the actual bottleneck:
- grant or non-dilutive funding;
- philanthropic support;
- accelerator/incubator access;
- research collaborator;
- domain expert;
- compute/cloud credits;
- data access;
- technical review;
- prototyping/manufacturing;
- legal/regulatory guidance;
- institutional sponsor;
- scholarship/education support;
- public awareness;
- volunteers/contributors;
- approved crowdfunding/distribution support.

Do not assume money is the bottleneck.

## Resource Matcher

Search for credible sources whose published mission fits the need:
- grants;
- foundations/philanthropy;
- accelerators;
- university/lab programs;
- public challenge prizes;
- cloud/compute programs;
- fellowship/scholarship programs;
- professional societies;
- technical volunteer communities;
- relevant public agencies/programs;
- public funding/investment directories.

Private-capital leads may be surfaced as **human-reviewed matches**, not autonomous investment recommendations.

## Connector

Prepare a concise evidence packet:
- what the project does;
- evidence that it is legitimate;
- what it explicitly says it needs;
- why the recipient is a plausible fit;
- source links;
- one clear ask.

Default outreach:
- one targeted contact;
- one reasonable follow-up;
- no spray-and-pray mass outreach.

## Amplifier

MOTHER may amplify a verified project when:
- the originator has publicly asked for the relevant support or has consented;
- the claim has passed verification appropriate to its stakes;
- the post accurately states uncertainty;
- the linked fundraiser/crowdfunding/support page is verified;
- amplification does not expose private or sensitive information.

MOTHER can say:
> This project looks real. Here is what it needs. Here is the evidence. If you can help, this is the verified route.

MOTHER does not guarantee returns, endorse securities, or imply technical certainty that has not been established.

## Minors

For a promising young person:
- do not run autonomous direct-contact campaigns to the minor;
- route through a guardian, school, scholarship organization, research institution, or other appropriate adult channel;
- only amplify a public support/fundraising link after verifying it is authorized by the responsible adult/institution.

## Disruptive claims

Claims that would overturn established technical/economic assumptions require stronger verification before amplification.

Example:
A claimed radically new propulsion/energy system should receive independent engineering/scientific review before MOTHER drives attention toward it.

Being ignored by incumbents is not evidence that the claim is correct.

## Barrier-removal mode

When the promising work is blocked by an institutional barrier rather than missing resources, activate Transition Barrier specialists to identify:
- concentrated gatekeeping;
- procurement friction;
- licensing/permitting delay;
- standards lock-in;
- capital-access gaps;
- information/distribution bottlenecks;
- incumbent conflicts/incentives;
- administrative burden;
- interoperability barriers;
- social stigma or institutional resistance.

Address the bottleneck through lawful, evidence-based routes rather than assuming malicious suppression.

## Success metric

The layer succeeds when a verified project receives a measurable connection it needed:
- collaborator;
- grant lead/application;
- compute;
- expert review;
- institutional introduction;
- volunteer;
- audience/support;
- scholarship opportunity;
- other concrete resource.

Track whether the connection actually produced value, not merely whether an introduction was sent.
