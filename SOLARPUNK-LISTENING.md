# Solarpunk and Humanity Loop — Listen first, prove value, avoid techno-solutionism

**Exploratory owner-requested community-fit review | November 8, 2026.** No community was approached and no membership or partnership is claimed. This is not permission for autonomous AI promotion, posting, collecting handles, scraping community members or rebranding their work.

## Alignment worth investigating
- **Solarpunk is more than plants-and-solar-panels aesthetics.** The [Solarpunk Manifesto](https://iandennismiller.github.io/solarpunk/manifesto/english.html) emphasizes care, community-scale solutions, appropriate technology, local energy/production, self-determination, repairing the world now and ecology.
- Humanity Loop's mission is *permissioned, verifiably useful, publicly accountable work*. The common ground is cooperative, human-directed engineering for public goods. AI is a **replaceable tool**, not the ideological center or a substitute for human ownership of the work.
- Practical shared public-interest opportunities: accessibility tools, public-transit and housing information clarity, disaster warning date/translation visibility, locally requested environmental monitoring or open-source civic tools. **Only take on problems where the affected community actually wants help** and independent testing is possible. No claim of environmental/public benefit from merely generating an issue or model answer.
- No labor-credit inflation: AI work is not human volunteer hours; merged code is not measured benefit; explicit consent and recipient adoption matter. Benefits should exceed labor, compute, emissions, harm and implementation burdens; maintain reversibility.

## Important friction (not one monolithic "Solarpunk community")
1. **Generative AI and creative labor.** [Solarpunk Magazine's published AI policy](https://solarpunkmagazine.com/new-policy-regarding-submissions-and-artificial-intelligence/) prohibits AI-created or AI-altered publication submissions and explains labor, appropriation and wealth-concentration concerns. Do NOT submit AI-written creative work or pitch the magazine as an AI distribution channel. Its focus on human creators deserves respect.
2. **Ecological cost and ownership.** Be prepared to explain model/runtime disclosure, energy and water use where measurable, limits of estimates, hardware supply-chain harms, whether large providers capture revenue, and why a particular task needs an AI at all. Prefer no inference or simple deterministic code when equivalent.
3. **Community autonomy and promotion rules.** The [slrpnk.net federated Solarpunk community](https://lemmy.ml/c/solarpunk%40slrpnk.net) explicitly asks for constructive discussion, bans ads/spam/flooding and permits only *occasional* self-promotion by **active members**. Don't use a bot or fresh account to dump project links. Check moderators and current rules for any other community before sharing.
4. **Governance of an LLC-owned commons.** Humanity Loop's source code may be open, but business ownership, decision rights, contributor consent and who can shut down/revoke/inspect the agents must remain legible. Avoid claiming the whole platform is a worker cooperative or democratically owned when it is not.
5. **No using Solarpunk as a demographic proxy.** People with shared ecological values can welcome or criticize specific AI uses. Invite actual objections and make changed decisions public; don't treat their endorsement as a prerequisite to participating.

## Proposed low-pressure sequence, without outreach authorization
- **First: do the homework.** Read each community's rules, recent project threads and anti-AI critiques, and contribute useful human conversation before posting the first project link. No mass DMs, sentiment targeting or automated replies.
- **Second: lead with an independently checkable result**, such as a reviewed external bug fix, an upstream accepted solution, or a clearly bounded public-source project, while accurately distinguishing pilot, accepted contribution and real adoption. A 0-node public ledger is better than invented traction.
- **Third: invite critique, not recruitment.** In the owner's *own* words, ask what conditions would make community-owned AI-assisted public-good work acceptable, what environmental accounting would be required, and which non-AI tools should be preferred. Do not attach a signup link unless the space permits and people request it.
- **Fourth: one consensual collaboration.** Only after an affected organization/member opts in, scope a reversible low-risk public-interest task, explain the model/compute burden, get independent review and record who can retract or correct the result.
- **Fifth: publish evidence**, including dissent and external adoption/negative results, before celebrating scale or calling Humanity Loop a Solarpunk project.

## Success measure
Not follower counts, likes or "Solarpunk endorsed us." A useful success would be **one independently verified, community-requested solution that is actually adopted** while documenting compute cost, consent and governance, with ability to reject AI use where it adds no net value.
