# Michigan SNAP time-limited work requirements: November 2026 waiver update versus older linked flyer and FAQ

**Observed:** 2026-11-10 UTC. **Status:** independently read official public pages and linked state PDF; **UNSENT** public-information reconciliation candidate. No applicant case, administrative determination, agency contact, corrected page, or outcome established.

## Verified source-to-source comparison

Michigan Department of Health and Human Services (MDHHS) issued a **2026-10-30 announcement** stating that a revised federal waiver ends for eight counties (Alger, Arenac, Iosco, Iron, Luce, Montmorency, Oceana, Roscommon) and **Bay City**, with time-limited SNAP work requirements taking effect there **November 1, 2026**. The state says the **continuing waiver** now covers seven counties (Alcona, Cheboygan, Mackinac, Ogemaw, Oscoda, Presque Isle, Schoolcraft) and five cities (Detroit, Eastpointe, Flint, Jackson, Saginaw). Its current main work-requirements page reproduces that narrower continuing-waiver list and says it applies until **October 31, 2027**.

However, the same main work-requirements page still links to **MDHHS-Pub-2209 (Rev. 3/26)**, an official PDF that lists **15** counties and **six** cities as waived under the March 1 phase. The eight removed counties and Bay City are explicitly included in that old flyer, and the PDF has **no visible November 2026 supersession warning** in the retrieved document text. The linked PDF is now materially stale for the affected locations. This is a dated publication, not proof that it was originally wrong.

The same current work-requirements page has an FAQ asking when **“these changes”** take effect and answering **“March 1, 2026.”** March 1 describes an earlier implementation phase, but it is ambiguous for readers of the newly updated November waiver list. The specific October 30 press release supplies the November 1 effective date.

A second MDHHS page, **“How does an able-bodied individual meet the work requirements?”**, still says a household with a child **under age 18** can qualify for an exception, while the current November guidance says **under age 14**. This separate linked page should be reconciled against current policy; do not presume every applicant's exception is determined solely by the web page.

The March date is an earlier true phase, but becomes misleading if interpreted as the effective date of the newest November change. Child-age difference is **under 18 vs under 14**.
