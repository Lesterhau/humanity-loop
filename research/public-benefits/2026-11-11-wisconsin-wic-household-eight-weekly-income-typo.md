# Wisconsin WIC 2026–27: eight-person weekly income table discrepancy

**Observed:** 2026-11-11 UTC, independent public-source review. **Status:** verified public-document numerical inconsistency; **unsent** editorial correction proposal, not an agency report, eligibility determination, or observed beneficiary outcome.

## Exact primary sources

1. Wisconsin Department of Health Services, [WIC: Apply for WIC](https://www.dhs.wisconsin.gov/wic/apply.htm), "WIC income guidelines" table, page last revised **October 8, 2026**. The current 2026–27 table's **household of 8** row prints **$1,938 weekly / $3,965 biweekly / $8,591 monthly / $103,082 annually**. Page states the income table is valid July 1, 2026–June 30, 2027.
2. Wisconsin DHS, linked [WIC Income Eligibility Table PDF](https://www.dhs.wisconsin.gov/wic/income-table.pdf), page 1 (5/2026), effective July 1, 2026–June 30, 2027. Its eight-person row **also prints $1,938 weekly**, with the same other three values. This is not a discrepancy between the state HTML and its linked PDF: both reproduce the suspect weekly number.
3. USDA Food and Nutrition Service, [2026–27 WIC income eligibility guidelines](https://fns-prod.azureedge.us/wic/fr-042926), published April 29, 2026, effective July 1, 2026. For the 48 contiguous states at 185% of the federal poverty guideline, its eight-person row is **$1,983 weekly / $3,965 biweekly / $8,591 monthly / $103,082 annually**.
4. USDA FNS [WIC FAQ, current income chart](https://www.fna.usda.gov/wic/faqs) independently reproduces the **$1,983** eight-person weekly amount for 2026–27.

A household of eight with **$1,950 gross income per week** falls above the published Wisconsin weekly figure but below USDA's weekly 185% guideline. This illustrates potential confusion for applicants relying on the Wisconsin weekly column. It does **not** establish that the household is eligible: Wisconsin could have state-specific policy, income calculation/period rules, adjunctive eligibility, residency and nutritional-risk conditions. The same Wisconsin row's matching annual/monthly/biweekly columns and the federal chart strongly suggest a **single-cell transcription error**, but a qualified Wisconsin WIC policy editor should confirm the controlling state standard before a public correction.
