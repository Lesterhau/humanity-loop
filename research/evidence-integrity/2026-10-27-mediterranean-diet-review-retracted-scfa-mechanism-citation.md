# Evidence Integrity — Mediterranean-diet mental-health review cites a subsequently retracted in-vitro mechanism study

**Source review:** 2026-10-27 UTC  
**Owner:** Humanity Loop main worker / evidence-integrity triage  
**Decision:** Verified narrow *background/mechanism citation* to a subsequently retracted study; **no verified included-study or pooled-outcome dependency**.  
**Risk:** Tier-0 public-source review; no editor contact, clinical advice, accusation, or public campaign authorized.

## Exact observation

Kabthymer et al., *Effect of Mediterranean diet on mental health outcomes: a systematic review*, *Nutrition Research Reviews* (published online **2025-11-06**, DOI **10.1017/S0954422425100243**), includes **104 adult observational/interventional studies** of Mediterranean-diet adherence and mental-health outcomes. In the separate **Potential mechanisms → Gut microbiota** narrative, the article says butyrate is noted for anti-inflammatory effects, citing references **[156–160]**. Reference **[159]** is Asarat et al. (2016), *Short-Chain Fatty Acids Regulate Cytokines and Th17/Treg Cells in Human Peripheral Blood Mononuclear Cells in vitro*, DOI **10.3109/08820139.2015.1122613**.

PubMed directly identifies the original Asarat article as **Retracted** and links the *Statement of Retraction*, DOI **10.1080/08820139.2026.2706222**, published online **2026-07-22**. The retraction postdates the November 2025 citing review. The notice's reason was **not** established in this bounded source review; do not infer misconduct or fabrication.

The citing article's eligibility criteria concern adult human diet/mental-health outcomes; the retracted paper is an **in-vitro human blood-cell immunology experiment**, not a Mediterranean-diet mental-health trial. Its appearance in a five-reference mechanism citation cluster is **not evidence that it is among the 104 included outcome studies**, and no effect on mental-health conclusions, treatment guidance, or clinical outcomes has been demonstrated.

## Novelty, alternative explanations, and falsification

- The review cited Asarat before the retraction existed; the chronology does **not** imply the authors knowingly relied on a withdrawn source.
- The anti-inflammatory mechanism sentence also cites four other sources. A later retraction of one source does not alone falsify that sentence or the review's observational/interventional results.
- The publisher may already have initiated a citation update not found in this bounded search; **absence of a discovered corrigendum is not proof none exists**.
- The related Evidence Integrity queue includes other citing reviews of the same Asarat paper. Each must be separately assessed; do not generalize dependency or inflate counts.
- **Falsification:** an exact publisher correction removing/replacing [159] resolves this narrow reader-facing citation concern; a verified included-study table showing Asarat as an outcome study would change classification, but conflicts with its in-vitro design.
