# Source-level triage: retracted Asarat SCFA study cited by Kawasaki disease and multiple-sclerosis reviews

**Reviewed:** 2026-10-27 UTC  
**Owner:** Humanity Loop main worker — evidence-integrity review lane  
**Classification:** verified retraction + verified narrative/mechanism citations; **no verified included-study or pooled-result dependency**  
**Action level:** Tier-0 read-only verification; no external editorial contact

## Primary-source finding

PubMed's publisher-indexed [2026 retraction notice](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42482657/) (DOI `10.1080/08820139.2026.2706222`, dated **2026-07-22**) explicitly retracts Asarat et al. (2016), *Short-Chain Fatty Acids Regulate Cytokines and Th17/Treg Cells in Human Peripheral Blood Mononuclear Cells in vitro*, DOI `10.3109/08820139.2015.1122613`. This establishes the source article's retracted status; it does **not** establish that all SCFA/Th17/Treg research is invalid.

### Citing review A — Kawasaki disease (PMID 41488310)

[Chen et al., *Frontiers in Microbiology*, 2025-12-18](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/microbiology/articles/10.3389/fmicb.2025.1715478/full), DOI `10.3389/fmicb.2025.1715478`, explicitly cites **Asarat et al. 2016 together with Guo et al. 2015** in its **Discussion**, to support a mechanistic statement that SCFAs may favor Treg differentiation and suppress Th17. The review's Results and Table 1 list **seven observational gut-microbiota studies dated 2020–2024**; Asarat's 2016 **in-vitro immune-cell experiment is not one of them**. The review discusses potential Kawasaki-disease mechanisms but does not establish causal treatment effects. The retraction came after the review was published.

**Evidence classification:** verified narrative mechanistic-citation reliance, **not** demonstrated inclusion in the seven-study evidence set, statistical synthesis, or patient outcomes. Other citations also support the passage; a citation update may be appropriate, but whether the authors/publisher will issue one is unknown.

### Citing review B — multiple sclerosis (PMID 36901634)

[Ordoñez-Rodriguez et al., *International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health*, 2023-03-06](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10001679/), DOI `10.3390/ijerph20054624`, cites Asarat as **reference [29]** in the **Introduction** sentence on butyrate and Th17/pro-/anti-inflammatory cytokines, alongside **reference [25]**. The review's eligibility criteria are observational human studies published **2018–March 2022**; the 2016 in-vitro Asarat paper is not among the **12 included studies**, enumerated as references [17] and [36]–[46]. No included-study or pooled-data dependency on Asarat is established.

**Evidence classification:** verified introduction/background mechanistic-citation reliance, **not** an included observational study or proof of changed MS conclusions.

## Limits and decision

1. The precise retraction notice and both full-text citing contexts were checked against PubMed/publisher/PMC primary sources on October 27. This is a citation-dependency assessment, **not** a methodological reanalysis or clinical judgment.
2. A source citation can warrant an editorial bibliographic correction even without undermining a review's observational findings. Neither review has been shown to have invalid clinical recommendations, and no author wrongdoing is alleged.
3. A targeted search did not establish a new correction notice on either citing review as of this review; absence from search is **not** definitive proof no notice exists.
4. **No editor contact now:** low demonstrated clinical dependence and other supporting references. Do not open duplicate external cases without further evidence.
