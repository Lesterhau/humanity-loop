## Sources and independent checks

1. **Publisher's primary retraction notice (2026-10-22):** [British Journal of Cancer, DOI 10.1038/s41416-026-03656-y](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41416-026-03656-y). The Editor-in-Chief retracted the 2014 laboratory study, [DOI 10.1038/bjc.2014.283](https://doi.org/10.1038/bjc.2014.283), citing overlapping sections within/between Figures 3B and 3C and loss of confidence in reported data. The notice reports non-response/unreachable authors; this record makes **no independent accusation** about intent.
2. **Citing synthesis, original full text:** Chen et al., *The association of statin therapy and cancer: a meta-analysis*, *Lipids in Health and Disease* (2023), [PMC10636977](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10636977), [PMID 37950285](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37950285), DOI 10.1186/s12944-023-01955-4. Its **Introduction** cites the now-retracted 2014 study as reference **3**, specifically for a proposed preclinical anti-angiogenic mechanism involving statins and bevacizumab.
3. **Scope check:** The 2023 synthesis reports **35 randomized controlled studies** and expressly excludes non-RCT/nonclinical studies from its quantitative synthesis. The retracted 2014 paper is a mechanistic laboratory study, and the citation occurs in the introductory narrative, not as an identified RCT in the pooled trial results. This supports **background-citation dependence**, not a conclusion that the pooled cancer-incidence/mortality estimates are invalid. We have not exhaustively reanalyzed all included trials.

## Risk and decision

- **Verified narrow issue:** Readers of the 2023 introduction can encounter a preclinical claim supported by a study formally retracted on 2026-10-22.
- **Not verified:** A material effect on the 2023 meta-analysis's quantitative conclusions, any author misconduct, or any current clinical recommendation requiring alteration.
- **Existing solution:** Publisher retraction notice is public. A linked notice or editorial clarification on the citing article would be the least intrusive reader-facing improvement, if the journal deems it appropriate.
- **Next action:** On 2026-11-06, check whether the citing article links the retraction or contains an editorial update. If still unaddressed and materially misleading, prepare **one** narrowly factual, non-accusatory inquiry to the citing journal for approval under Humanity Loop's outreach limits. No email sent in this cycle.
- **Falsifiers / closure:** Evidence that the journal already flags the retraction, that reference 3 has been replaced, or that the contextual claim does not rely on that paper. Closure requires source-level verification.
- **10th Man — If this idea wins, who loses?** A sensational report could falsely imply statins are unsafe or that 35 RCTs were retracted, harming public understanding and researchers' reputations. Keep the correction proportional to the **single preclinical citation**; credit the journal/publisher for any actual correction.
