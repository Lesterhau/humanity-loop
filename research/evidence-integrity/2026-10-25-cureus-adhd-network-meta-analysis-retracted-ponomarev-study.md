# Source-level evidence check — 2026-10-25

## Finding (bounded)

**A 2026 Cureus network meta-analysis lists a study retracted later in 2026 among its included trials.** This establishes study-level inclusion in the review's published evidence inventory, **not** the numerical influence on pooled network estimates, an invalid review, author wrongdoing, or clinical impact.

- **Citing review:** Hassan Awaji H. H. et al., *Mapping the Mind: A Network Meta-Analysis of Mindfulness and Traditional and Digital Interventions for Cognitive and Behavioral Enhancement in Children With ADHD*, *Cureus* 18(1), e102453, **published January 28, 2026**, DOI **10.7759/cureus.102453**. Its methods/results report **16 included studies involving 806 participants**.
- **Included original:** Ponomarev R., Sklyar S., Krasilnikova V., Savina T., *Digital Cognitive Training for Children with Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder*, *Journal of Psycholinguistic Research* 52:2303–2319 (2023), DOI **10.1007/s10936-023-10003-2**. The original describes a randomized comparison involving **90 children**, ages 8–10, of DBT-based digital cognitive mindfulness training versus control.
- **Exact match in citing review:** the published **Table 1, spanning PDF pages 5–6**, explicitly lists **“Ponomarev R et al. [21]” (2023)** as an RCT, 90 participants (45/45), digital cognitive mindfulness/DBT-based intervention versus waitlist control, with Conners-3 and executive-function assessments. **Reference [21]** names the original study and exact DOI. The same review's **Table 2** includes a “DBT Mindfulness” intervention ranking, but this check has **not** verified which exact trial effect-size rows generated that ranking or its sensitivity to exclusion.
- **Retraction:** the original publisher marks the 2023 article **RETRACTED** and dates the retraction **October 23, 2026**; separate publisher notice DOI **10.1007/s10936-026-10324-y**, published October 23. The retraction **postdates** the January 2026 review. The notice's full rationale is subscription-gated in the source reviewed here; do not invent its detailed grounds.

## Interpretation, uncertainty, and non-duplication

The review was published about nine months **before** the retraction; the reviewers could not have known of this later notice when publishing. A later retraction merits checking whether the study's effect data influenced any network comparison or ranking, but **the published Table 1 alone cannot establish the quantitative effect of removing it**. It is not appropriate to tell families or clinicians that mindfulness/DBT interventions are ineffective or that this meta-analysis is false.

This is a **different citing review** from Humanity Loop's existing issue #25 (Liu et al. 2026 Frontiers Bayesian mindfulness meta-analysis), even though both concern the **same Ponomarev 2023 retraction**. De-duplicate at the study/retraction level and separately assess each review's dependency. No evidence of a publisher correction for this exact Cureus review was independently established in this pass; absence is not proved.

**10th-Man question — if a correction campaign succeeds, who loses?** Review authors could suffer unfair reputational harm if a retrospective citation issue is presented as wrongdoing; readers could be misled if an unquantified dependency is exaggerated. Use a neutral, source-linked review and proportionate editorial inquiry only after an independent domain/effect-data check.
