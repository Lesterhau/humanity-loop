## Problem and existing response

UNICEF's 13 October Nepal situation report documented 30,000 children needing education assistance after the August floods; as of 9 October, UNICEF had recorded 1,481 children accessing formal/nonformal education against its own 5,000 response target. These figures are *not* a claim that all remaining children lack services from all actors. UNICEF/Government, Education Cannot Wait, and other groups already run substantial recovery programs. [UNICEF report](https://www.unicef.org/media/185056/file/Nepal-Humanitarian-SitRep-13-October-2026.pdf.pdf); [ECW commitment, 15 September](https://www.educationcannotwait.org/news-stories/press-releases/education-cannot-wait-commits-us1-million-sustain-hope-and-learning).

The Nepal Education Ministry separately announced a **Scholarship Bank** and a private-school-operator **pledge** of NPR 1 billion for 1,000 affected students. The September 10 report said detailed rules were still forthcoming; neither a pledge nor a policy announcement proves disbursement. This is a significant duplication/coordination check before creating another funding pipeline. [Kathmandu Post, 10 September](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2026/09/10/nepal-announces-scholarship-bank-for-children-orphaned-or-displaced-by-floods).

## Candidate A — The Hundred Nepal ↔ MIT Group Foundation (priority for fit verification)

**Demand/coordination side, independently published:** [The Hundred Nepal](https://thehundrednepal.org/) is a diaspora-led cohort-education pledge effort, reporting **222 pledgers and over US$29,000/year pledged, not collected**, as of the public page reviewed 24 October. Its [1 October update](https://thehundrednepal.org/reports/update-1-october/) says it is selecting **one registered delivery partner by year-end**, has joined Project Kuhiro, and is evaluating school fees, ancillary attendance costs, child protection, and long-term support. Its own public list of groups already considered names Project Kuhiro, Chakraviu Nepal and Samaanta Foundation, but **does not list MIT Group Foundation**. Absence from the list is not proof that the groups have not spoken.

**Potential delivery/oversight side, independently published:** [MIT Group Foundation's Nepal Flood 2026 program](https://www.mitgf.org/projects/nepal-flood-2026) describes an **18 September MOU with Nepal's National Child Rights Council**, an intended program for **up to 110 eligible children through Grade 12**, NCRC-led beneficiary verification, safeguarding and non-duplication review, and Foundation-managed financial/technical support. Its site publicly invites financial support for this initiative. These are **the Foundation's documented program descriptions and commitments**, not independently verified beneficiary enrollment, implementation or funds disbursed.

**Why it could fit:** same affected geography/event; multi-year education continuity; institutional child-safeguarding/verification arrangements; an explicitly open partner-selection effort and an existing organization seeking support. **Why it may not:** The Hundred seeks a single *registered delivery partner* and could prefer a locally operating Nepal NGO, whereas MIT is a funder/program coordinator; MIT's 110 planned places may already be fully funded or covered by government/private scholarships; no evidence either side wants this introduction. The Hundred's pledges are not cash, and there is no permission to direct them.

**Required gate before even proposing outreach:** (1) verify MIT's role as eligible local delivery partner versus external funder, (2) verify whether The Hundred already considered MIT, (3) check overlap with the government Scholarship Bank/NCRC and whether additional unserved students or non-duplicative expenses exist, (4) obtain human review for a narrowly factual optional organizational introduction. No beneficiary identities or records should be requested or transferred.

## Candidate B — The Hundred Nepal ↔ United Community Nepal (secondary; narrower operational fit)

[United Community Nepal (UniCoN)](https://unitedcommunitynepal.org/the-organization/) states Nepal registration no. 4442 and Social Welfare Council affiliation no. 53686; [GlobalGiving lists it as vetted since 2025](https://www.globalgiving.org/donate/103958/united-community-nepal/). UniCoN reports direct education-recovery work in Nuwakot and Dhading, including school materials for around 400 children, and its [GlobalGiving flood-school project](https://www.globalgiving.org/projects/nepal-flood-emergency-help-families-survive/) seeks funds to restore two schools and sustain schooling. GlobalGiving project reports are **submitted by the project leader**, not independent verification of every field outcome.

**Possible fit:** registered local implementation capacity, affected districts, school-continuity work, public donation route. **Major mismatch:** UniCoN's current published appeal emphasizes infrastructure and school operating costs, whereas The Hundred is designing multi-year *individual-cohort* educational support and partner selection. No evidence of existing child-level case-management/safeguarding capacity for that cohort or of consent to a partnership. Treat as secondary only if the first option fails and The Hundred's criteria include this operating model.

## Falsification, risk, and decision

- **Falsifiers:** a published The Hundred partner shortlist already includes the candidate; MIT's program is fully funded/not seeking partners; NCRC/government schemes already cover the same cohort and costs; UniCoN cannot meet long-term child-protection/reporting requirements; partner selection criteria exclude the organizations; or a newer official needs assessment changes the opportunity.
- **10th Man:** A well-intentioned outside introduction could duplicate scholarships, displace local organizations, fragment safeguarding records, or encourage donor competition for the same children. A responsible match must be additive and controlled by local child-protection authorities.
- **Decision:** One **plausible organizational pairing for due diligence** (A), one **lower-fit alternate** (B). **Zero qualified/accepted matches, zero correspondence, zero donations, zero child-level data, zero verified downstream benefit.** No outreach or public promotional post from this research alone.

Do not contact minors or solicit donations.
