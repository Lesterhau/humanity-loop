## Primary-source crosswalk (publication date is not surveillance cutoff)

1. **WHO, Weekly Epidemiological Record vol. 101 issue 44, published 6 November 2026; surveillance through 27 September 2026:** https://www.who.int/publications/journals/weekly-epidemiological-record/wer101-44/ . WHO Table 1 reports Chad **867 cases / 42 deaths, 4.8% reported case-fatality ratio (CFR)** for 1 January–27 September; **168 cases / 22 deaths, 13.1% reported CFR** in its **last-28-day** column. September country summary reports a **64% fall in cases** but **100% rise in deaths** relative to August. These are *reported surveillance ratios*, not infection-fatality risks, case-cohort follow-up, a proof of deteriorating clinical quality, or today's situation. WHO explicitly cautions that definitions, underreporting, deaths outside facilities and reporting delays make comparisons difficult. The published 6 November issue contains September data, **not November incidence**.
2. **Cross-border comparison in the SAME WHO table and cutoff:** Cameroon **3,018 cases / 90 deaths (3.0%) cumulative**, **585 / 17 (2.9%) last 28 days**; Nigeria **72,374 / 467 (0.6%) cumulative**, **3,111 / 28 (0.9%) last 28 days**. These are not directly comparable measures of health-system performance, and country-level figures cannot locate a sanitation problem at one village or camp.
3. **UNICEF West and Central Africa French release, 12 August 2026:** https://www.unicef.org/wca/fr/communiqu%C3%A9s-de-presse/alors-que-le-chol%C3%A9ra-se-propage-au-del%C3%A0-des-fronti%C3%A8res-gilles-fagninou . Describes a Lake Chad cross-border transmission corridor, flooding/WASH risks, disproportionate child exposure, and existing UNICEF/government response. Its **>50,000 Nigeria cases / 338 deaths** figure reflects an earlier **end-July** snapshot; it is not a contradiction of the later WHO 27 September **72,374 / 467** counts. Do not describe the August article as a currently inaccurate live bulletin.
4. **WHO–KSrelief, 22 September 2026:** https://www.afro.who.int/news/ksrelief-and-who-sign-us-53-million-agreement-combat-cholera-lake-chad-basin . A **US$5,327,244.97 signed implementation agreement** covering Cameroon, Chad, Mali, Niger and Nigeria includes WASH and surveillance. Signing alone does not establish funds disbursed, installations completed, which sites were served, or any change in mortality.
5. **UNICEF Chad, 23 October 2026:** https://www.unicef.org/chad/fr/recits/%C2%AB-nous-ne-savons-pas-combien-de-temps-nous-pourrons-encore-tenir-dans-ces-conditions-%C2%BB-howa . The October 26 Humanity Loop review already logged **163 shared latrine/shower cabins reportedly benefiting 3,260** at Fourkoulom and remaining stated needs; the new WHO country-level surveillance report does **not** independently confirm whether those precise latrine needs persist or have been addressed.
6. **WHO Chad French country bulletin directory, October 2026:** https://www.afro.who.int/fr/countries/chad/publication/bulletin-hebdomadaire-integre-des-urgences-de-sante-publique-au-tchad-2026 . Lists a week-40 PDF dated **19 October**. The index was opened; **underlying week-40 figures were not extracted or reconciled**. It is the highest-priority next official source to inspect before using the older 27 September data to target any response.

## Decision: real risk context, no new intervention mandate

**Verified signal:** WHO's 6 November publication makes the *reported last-28-day Chad death-to-case ratio* (22/168, 13.1%) visible, despite fewer reported cases. This warrants a carefully bounded check of the newer country bulletin, treatment-access information and surveillance definitions. It does **not** establish that anyone was denied treatment, that one locality has 13.1% fatality, that the 2026 UNICEF response failed, or that Humanity Loop should create an aid provider.

**Existing solutions:** Ministry of Public Health and Prevention, WHO country/regional response, UNICEF Cameroon/Chad and local implementation partners, plus the signed WHO–KSrelief programme. No verified partner invitation for a zero-cash translation/data-quality task was found in these sources.

**Novelty/usefulness/evidence (heuristic, not impact metrics):** novelty 0.35 (newly published crosswalk of existing outbreak); usefulness 0.70 (avoids misleading stale figures and targets due verification); evidence 0.88 (official WHO/UNICEF sources, but no direct current site or health-facility audit); uncertainty 0.55 (reporting/denominator/local coverage).

**STEEP+/TIPPO:** social (displacement and children), technological (surveillance reporting), economic (WASH funding/maintenance), environmental (flooding and water), political/legal (cross-border public health coordination), values (equitable access); time horizon H1 current outbreak/H2 durable WASH. **Futures triangle:** push of seasonal outbreaks/displacement; pull of safe water and rapid treatment; weight of insecure access and resource constraints. Cross-impact: an apparent decline in cases can coexist with high reported deaths and delayed detection; do not infer causation.

**Falsification and next due check (by 17 November 2026 UTC):** Read WHO Chad's **week-40 19 October bulletin PDF** and any later official Ministry/WHO update. Determine whether Chad's **22/168** WHO last-28-day figures use reporting windows/case definitions comparable to newer local counts; identify whether a named local response owner has publicly requested *specific* voluntary translation, accessibility, or verification assistance. Recheck whether Fourkoulom and Nguéli/Kousséri site-specific WASH shortfalls persist from dated local reports. If no newer site evidence or request, retain as `needs-monitoring / no-new-intervention`. Qualified French/public-health editorial review before any dissemination; **separate owner permission** before contact.
