## Specific question and evidence
Could Humanity Loop identify a novel, bounded, publicly useful intervention around rapid AI-compute power swings and grid reliability, distinct from the [2026-10-27 grid-flexibility review](2026-10-27-global-en-ai-infra-grid-flexibility-no-signal.md)?

1. **Actual observed system event — PJM, 2026-08-11.** PJM's own report documents the July 22 Virginia incident in which about 3,800 MW of computational load transferred off the grid after a line fault, producing voltage/frequency disturbances. Operators restored balance; PJM says no significant reliability impacts occurred. PJM and Dominion were already evaluating ride-through requirements. The July 16 FERC order required NERC to submit first-phase computational-load standards by 2026-12-31; PJM expected specific voltage/frequency ride-through standards in 2027. Source: https://insidelines.pjm.com/pjm-dominion-review-large-load-transfer-event/ . This is evidence of a real risk and active institutional ownership, **not** evidence that a particular data center is currently unsafe or that any party was negligent.
2. **Established research response — Ko and Zhu, IEEE Transactions on Power Systems, published 2026-04-20.** A peer-reviewed simulation study describes how periodic AI workload power changes can excite grid oscillatory modes in modeled power systems; results depend on system strength, siting and workload conditions. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1109/TPWRS.2026.3685506 ; public author version: https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.16457 . Simulated outcomes are not proof of an actual event's root cause.
3. **Recent distinct mechanism — Joshi et al., arXiv preprint submitted 2026-10-23.** The authors model a converter-efficiency penalty for a specified oscillating GPU-rack load using measured component efficiency curves, reporting 0.88 percentage-point lower conversion efficiency in a two-stage example and 1.66 percentage points with point-of-load conversion. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33300 . **Not independently replicated or established as a typical fleet-wide loss**; this is a single modeled scenario, not a general estimate of AI electricity waste.
4. **Additional model-development response — Lin et al., arXiv preprint submitted 2026-10-14.** A composite model explicitly distinguishes IT, cooling and auxiliary branches and simulates protection/reconnection behavior. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19565 . The authors describe existing WECC PERC1 work and other dynamic-model studies, further reducing confidence that the modeling gap is unaddressed.

## Novelty, foresight and falsification
- **STEEP+:** Technology (converter dynamics and modeling); Environment (possible conversion losses); Economics (electricity-system costs); Social (service reliability); Political/legal (NERC/FERC standards and utility planning); ethics (avoid falsely attributing grid faults to named facilities).
- **TIPPOs:** Trend: denser synchronized compute; issue: fast power changes and ride-through; plans: PJM/NERC requirements; projection: more dynamic modeling; obstacle: limited independently published field data and deployment-specific telemetry.
- **Three Horizons:** H1 existing ride-through standards and grid operation; H2 dynamic workload-aware modeling and hardware buffers; H3 possible jointly designed compute/grid controls (speculative).
- **Futures Triangle:** Push: AI load growth and July event; pull: stable, efficient grid-interactive compute; weight: historical large-load planning and proprietary operational telemetry.
- **Negative-space question:** Can a public, privacy-safe benchmark compare **measured** rack-level conversion efficiency and fast grid-facing load dynamics under realistic mixed workloads? This remains an *unverified need*, not a ready project.
- **Falsification criteria:** An independently replicated benchmark, existing open dataset with sufficient measurements, or published standards already covering the same evaluation would weaken a proposed new Humanity Loop benchmark. Conversely, a maintainer's explicit request for a small, license-compatible documentation or reproducibility contribution could justify a bounded Tier-0 task.
