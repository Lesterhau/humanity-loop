## Verified public-interest facts

- The Horse Protection Act prohibits soring: intentionally causing pain or distress to a horse's limbs to alter its gait; the law regulates horse shows, exhibitions, sales and auctions.
- On Nov 13, APHIS opened a public-comment period on a **new proposal** following court action affecting the 2024 final rule. The new proposal would withdraw surviving 2024 provisions and revise 9 CFR part 11, but **the proposal itself has not changed the current rules**.
- Proposed inspection measures include more training and conflict-of-interest safeguards for industry-linked Designated Qualified Persons (DQPs), more standardized inspections, sampling for prohibited substances, and measures against masking agents. The Federal Register discusses proposed APHIS dual-veterinarian concurrence and immediate appeal to an independent qualified Appeals Official before notification to event management in certain cases.
- The proposal would remove the existing scar rule, citing judicial/legal concerns, while continuing to use the statute's definition of a sore horse. **Do not claim removal of the scar rule is already in force solely because it is proposed.**
- The proposal would clarify that a sore horse is disqualified from the relevant class rather than automatically from an entire event. APHIS identifies welfare enforcement, fair competition and procedural fairness as competing design objectives.
- The full economic analysis and proposed regulatory-flexibility analysis are identified in the Federal Register; this review did **not** independently assess the underlying cost model or the empirical sensitivity/specificity of the inspection techniques.

## Neutral civic-information draft (NOT distributed)

**A federal horse-welfare rule is open for public comment through December 14, 2026.** USDA APHIS is proposing changes to how inspectors identify horse soring at regulated events, including inspector training, substance testing and an appeals process. The proposal responds to court decisions affecting a 2024 rule. It is **not yet final**, so readers should not assume these measures already apply. The official proposal and supporting documents are available under docket **APHIS-2026-0067**.
