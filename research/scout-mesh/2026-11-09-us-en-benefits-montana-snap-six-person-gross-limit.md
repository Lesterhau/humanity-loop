## Primary sources and exact observations

1. **Montana DPHHS, SNAP Program Overview**, https://dphhs.mt.gov/hcsd/SNAP/ . Public web reader retrieved the state's official page on 2026-11-09; the reader described the indexed copy as crawled about one week earlier. Under **Financial Criteria → SNAP INCOME LIMITS**, it labels its chart **“October 1, 2025 through September 30, 2026.”** However, the displayed standard 130% gross/net values for one person ($1,729 / $1,330), two ($2,345 / $1,804), five ($4,191 / $3,224), seven ($5,421 / $4,170), and eight ($6,037 / $4,644) match the *following* fiscal year, 2026-10-01 through 2027-09-30. In **“All Other Households”**, the **six-person** row uniquely says **$1,806 gross / $3,697 net**. The adjacent five-person gross limit is $4,191 and seven-person gross limit $5,421. The page's separate Expanded Categorical Eligibility table lists $7,394 gross for six people, which is a different eligibility category and must not be conflated with the standard 130% table.
2. **USDA Food and Nutrition Administration, SNAP Eligibility**, https://fns-prod.azureedge.us/snap/recipient/eligibility . Retrieved via the federal official site on 2026-11-09; the page states **updated November 3, 2026** and expressly covers **October 1, 2026 through September 30, 2027**. Its federal table for the contiguous states (including Montana) lists the six-person **130% gross monthly income limit as $4,806**, net as $3,697. Five-person $4,191 and seven-person $5,421 corroborate the sequence.
3. **Nevada Division of Social Services, current SNAP income charts**, https://www.dss.nv.gov/access-nv/eligibility-payments-manual/income-limit-charts/ . Independent state-government cross-check retrieved 2026-11-09, explicitly effective **October 1, 2026**; six-person 130% gross **$4,806**, net **$3,697**. Nevada's own state categorical-eligibility arrangements do not establish Montana's categorical eligibility rules; only the federal standard row is cross-checked here.

## Findings

- **F1 — materially incorrect printed figure:** Montana's six-person *standard gross* row displays **$1,806** where USDA's applicable FY2027 standard is **$4,806**. Difference **$3,000 per month**. The Montana table's own neighboring rows and the correct six-person net amount reinforce that this is likely a transcription error. A six-person household reading the *non-categorically eligible* row could wrongly conclude its income is too high to qualify for an initial SNAP assessment. Actual eligibility depends on other criteria and deductions; do **not** infer a denial, financial loss, agency processing error, or number of affected applicants.
- **F2 — period-label conflict:** Montana's chart heading says **FY2026 (Oct 2025–Sep 2026)** while the numbers otherwise correspond to **FY2027 (Oct 2026–Sep 2027)**. The correct fix must confirm which table and program year the agency intends, rather than blindly relabeling all tables. The standard six-person row and date label can be addressed together after state editorial verification.
- **F3 — category caveat:** The Montana page separately offers expanded categorical eligibility standards. A higher threshold can apply to eligible households, so **$4,806 is not a universal Montana SNAP cutoff**, and $1,806 should not be used for any eligibility advice. Applicants should use the official application/helpline and request an actual determination.

USDA's current FY2027 standard for six people in the contiguous states is **$4,806** (net $3,697), which matches the adjacent values in your table. The same section is headed “October 1, 2025 through September 30, 2026” even though its numbers appear to be FY2027 values. Could the program publications team verify the live page, confirm the intended period and eligibility category, and correct the public chart if necessary? This is a request to verify published guidance, not an allegation about eligibility determinations.
