## Proposed short public-information clarification (NOT published)

> WHO's November 13 polio emergency statement uses surveillance data available as of September 15. A country appearing in a WHO poliovirus category does not necessarily mean people there have developed paralytic polio or that local transmission has been demonstrated. For example, UKHSA's July update describes the UK's March wastewater detection without evidence of local transmission. WHO distinguishes travel-vaccination recommendations for countries with local transmission of wild/type 1 or type 3 poliovirus from those for type 2 vaccine-derived poliovirus. If travel is urgent, WHO says an indicated polio vaccine dose can still be given by departure in the relevant local-transmission category, although this does not guarantee immediate protection or automatic acceptance of travel documents. Check current country-specific exit rules and individual vaccination needs with official public-health/travel services.

**Clinical and regulatory review required:** A qualified travel-health editor should check the phrase “indicated” and distinguish vaccination advice, WHO state-level temporary recommendations, and actually enforced country-specific border rules. Do not interpret this as an instruction to self-administer a vaccine, an individual medical assessment, or proof of current visa/entry/exit requirements.
