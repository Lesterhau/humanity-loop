## Sources independently reviewed (October 30–31 UTC)

1. [UNICEF Innocenti, *Drivers of Learning in Differentiated Classrooms* (September 2026), full 55-page working paper](https://www.unicef.org/innocenti/media/13566/file/UNICEF-Innocenti-Learning-Enhancement-Strategy-Bangladesh-Report-2026.pdf). Primary research on Bangladesh's Learning Enhancement Strategy (LES), developed with the Directorate of Secondary and Higher Education (DSHE) and National Curriculum and Textbook Board (NCTB). The study covers 24 pilot secondary schools in 2024–25. It reports that 91% of assessed Grade 6 students were below 50% correct on Grade 5 mathematics questions at baseline and 65% were below 50% in Bangla. In pooled descriptive results, average scores changed from 19% to 26% in mathematics and 43% to 47% in Bangla. **These changes cannot be attributed causally to LES.**
2. [UNICEF Innocenti publication overview](https://www.unicef.org/innocenti/reports/drivers-learning-differentiated-classrooms) — describes differentiated assessment as the practice most consistently *associated* with better outcomes, alongside materials and teacher-support constraints. The original working paper's methodological limitations (pp. 51–52) explicitly acknowledge no comparison schools in years 1–2, nonrandom attrition, a small 24-school sample, imperfect cross-round test comparability and self-reported practices. Approximately 47% of students with valid baseline tests were missing at endline in the pooled comparison, with inverse-probability weighting used; no causal effect size should be invented.
3. [Bangladesh government's DSHE Learning Acceleration in Secondary Education (LAISE) project](https://laise.gov.bd/page/read/207), publicly accessible on October 31. It describes an ongoing 2023–28 government/World Bank-backed effort with planned formative assessment and remediation across secondary institutions, teacher training, curriculum/content and monitoring components. **LAISE and the UNICEF/DSHE LES pilot are related efforts, not interchangeable programs.** Its project targets and described progress are agency statements, not independent outcome verification.
4. [UNICEF Bangladesh education overview](https://www.unicef.org/bangladesh/en/education) documents broader barriers to literacy, numeracy and completion. It is context for need, not evidence of a newly discovered service outage.

## Evaluation: need, novelty, tractability and existing solutions

- **Verified need:** Major foundational skills gaps, low observed proficiency, difficulty sustaining differentiated assessment, and shortages of levelled learning materials in the sampled settings. The UNICEF report's teacher accounts and regression associations support investigating these barriers; they do not show that an independent Humanity Loop intervention would improve student outcomes.
- **Existing implementation:** LES is already run by DSHE/NCTB and UNICEF. The report states that the 2026 year-three plan includes a revised toolkit, a matched intervention/control evaluation with 30 additional intervention schools, digitized diagnostic assessment, master trainers and ongoing support. **Plans are not proof of completed 2026 implementation or effectiveness.** The distinct LAISE project also has funded government structures for teacher training, formative assessment and remediation. Building another generic education app, unsolicited teaching package, learner data collection system or unvalidated AI tutor would likely duplicate existing work and create privacy/quality risks.
- **Novelty/deduplication:** This September 2026 study was not the subject of the previously checked Humanity Loop South Asia Scouts, which examined Nepal flood education, Pakistan school safety, general digital access, Afghanistan girls' access and UGC scholarship navigation. The finding is distinct research, but the underlying problem and intervention ownership are already public and active.
