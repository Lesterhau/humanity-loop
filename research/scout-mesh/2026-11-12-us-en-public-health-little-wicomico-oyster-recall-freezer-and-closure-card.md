# Little Wicomico River oyster recall — frozen-product and continued-closure action card

**Observation:** 2026-11-12 UTC. **Lane:** US-EN public-health / food-safety Scout. **Status:** primary-source-checked, public-safe **UNSENT** human-review draft. **No external outreach or distribution.**

## Verified official evidence

1. **Virginia Department of Health (VDH), [November 2026 response page](https://www.vdh.virginia.gov/news/salmonella-outbreak-oysters/):** The Little Wicomico River harvesting closure was **extended effective November 8, 2026**, beyond the original October 9–November 7 emergency period, because oyster samples from October 13 and October 21 tested positive for Salmonella. The page also reports **3 of 8** November 2 oyster-meat samples positive; November 9 samples and November 4 UNC samples are **pending**, not negative. VDH states reopening requires **three consecutive rounds of negative product testing**. Other Virginia growing areas are not covered by this specific closure.
2. **VDH [recall notice dated October 9](https://www.vdh.virginia.gov/environmental-health/shellfish-safety/recall-little-wicomico-river/):** Voluntary recall covers **shellstock oysters** from Little Wicomico Oyster Company **VA-1421-SP**, harvested in **Little Wicomico River or Little Wicomico River Lower, VA, September 18–October 8, 2026**. VDH instructed receiving states and downstream businesses to stop sale/service and remove recalled inventory.
3. **FDA [safety alert, updated October 30](https://www.fda.gov/food/alerts-advisories-safety-information/safety-alert-salmonella-oysters-little-wicomico-river-virginia):** The recalled oysters were distributed to restaurants/retailers in **CA, CO, CT, DC, DE, FL, GA, IL, MD, MO, NC, NJ, NV, NY, PA, TX and VA**, with possible further distribution. FDA warns that the original oysters are past normal shelf life but **previously frozen** recalled product can remain in consumers' and businesses' freezers. Distribution states are **not** the same thing as outbreak-case states.
4. **CDC [public action notice, updated October 30](https://www.cdc.gov/salmonella/outbreaks/oysters-10-26/index.html) and [investigation](https://www.cdc.gov/salmonella/outbreaks/oysters-10-26/investigation.html):** CDC reported **28 cases across 13 states**, **5 hospitalizations among 22 cases with hospitalization information**, and **0 reported deaths** as of October 30. These are dated investigation totals, not current November 12 counts. CDC says to discard/return any recalled frozen oysters, clean contacted surfaces, and seek medical care for severe illness. The outbreak investigation is ongoing in the last checked official material.
5. **VDH [condemnation order 010-105](https://www.vdh.virginia.gov/environmental-health/shellfish-safety/shellfish-closures/shellfish-area-condemnation-010-105/), effective November 8:** The specified Little Wicomico River shellfish-harvesting area remains legally closed; the order describes a broader **harvesting restriction on shellfish** than the **specific previously distributed oyster recall**. Neither is a ban on all Virginia oysters or drinking water.

## Draft consumer / retailer card — NOT DISTRIBUTED

**Check frozen oysters from Little Wicomico River.** A recall of Little Wicomico Oyster Company shellstock oysters harvested **September 18–October 8, 2026** remains relevant if any product was frozen. Check the shellstock tag, harvest area/date and supplier; do **not eat, sell or serve** recalled oysters. Discard or return them and clean any surfaces or equipment that touched them. If you cannot identify the source of potentially recalled frozen oysters, ask the retailer/distributor before using them. FDA's recall scope covers specific harvests, **not all oysters from Virginia**.

Virginia has **extended its Little Wicomico River shellfish harvesting closure from November 8 onward**. Do not confuse the closure's ongoing status with the shelf life of previously distributed oysters.

Seek medical care promptly for severe diarrhea, bloody diarrhea, fever over 102°F with diarrhea, persistent vomiting or dehydration; see [CDC's full symptom guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/salmonella/outbreaks/oysters-10-26/index.html). This card is not a substitute for medical advice.
