## Source review (primary institutional sources)

1. **International Energy Agency (IEA), _Powering Artificial Intelligence_**, published **2026-10-14**, https://www.iea.org/reports/powering-artificial-intelligence . The IEA reports surveying policy/regulatory approaches in 34 jurisdictions representing about 90% of global data-centre capacity. It already provides a policy framework addressing affordability, energy security, emissions and water. This is an *existing solution landscape*, not evidence that all jurisdictions have implemented the recommendations.
2. **UN Economic Commission for Europe (UNECE), _Aligning Data Centres and Large Loads Growth With Grid Resilience_**, published **September 2026**, https://unece.org/info/publications/pub/416640 ; accompanying **2026-09-08** public explanation: https://unece.org/media/press/416181 . UNECE explicitly identifies incomplete implementation of conditional connections, demand response, fair cost allocation, lifecycle water/carbon reporting, and local grid planning. These are recognized gaps with existing institutional owners, not newly discovered defects at a named facility.
3. **IEA, _Electricity 2026_, executive summary**, https://www.iea.org/reports/electricity-2026/executive-summary . The IEA estimates that **750–900 GW of projects in grid queues across categories** could potentially be enabled by flexible/non-firm connection agreements. **This is a modeled global opportunity for queued projects, NOT 750–900 GW of data-centre capacity, actual unlocked capacity, or a guarantee for any specific network.** The IEA expressly notes site-specific voltage, short-circuit, substation and demand-profile constraints.
4. **UNECE, October 20 2026 discussion forum** on data centres, water and energy: https://unece.org/sustainable-energy/events/energy-efficiency-bimonthly-open-discussion-forum-series-20.10.2026 . The forum is additional evidence of an existing expert venue for the same cross-sector coordination question, not evidence of a completed intervention.

**Access qualification:** The IEA overview and executive-summary text were directly inspected. UNECE public search-indexed publication/press descriptions were reviewed, but some direct UNECE page fetches returned HTTP 403 in this session; no claim is made to have inspected inaccessible underlying full text.

## Candidate hypothesis and skeptical check

**Hypothesis considered:** There is an under-served public-interest opportunity for Humanity Loop to develop a new generic data-centre grid-flexibility / water-cost disclosure checklist.

**Existing-solutions check:** The IEA and UNECE already articulate closely overlapping principles, report categories and implementation pathways. A generic checklist would likely duplicate their work. The review found **no independently verified jurisdiction-specific omitted disclosure, erroneous public guidance, interested maintainer request, or named partner** that could support a bounded nonduplicative contribution today.

**10th-Man counterargument:** A useful implementation gap may remain even with mature international frameworks; a small local translation, comparison or performance-data validation could eventually help. But selecting a target without public evidence and an accountable receiving institution would be premature and could misrepresent forecast values as realized capacity.

## STEEP+, TIPPOs and horizons

- **STEEP+:** Technological (rapidly varying AI loads, storage and demand response); Economic (network cost allocation, household affordability); Environmental (power generation, cooling water and land impacts); Political/Legal (connection rules and disclosure); Social/Values/Ethical (who bears costs and outage risk); Demographic (locally concentrated communities).
- **TIPPOs:** Trend = rising large-load concentration; Issue = grid readiness, cost and water trade-offs; Problem = variable jurisdiction-level execution; Possibility = independently audited conditional-connection and impact reporting; Opportunity = only if a specific under-served, evidence-backed case emerges.
- **Three Horizons:** H1 conventional firm-load interconnection and fragmented reporting; H2 conditional connections and cross-sector disclosure pilots; H3 transparent, verified large-load flexibility with equitable cost and water accounting.
- **Futures Triangle:** Push = rising demand and grid constraints; Pull = reliable affordable low-impact electricity; Weight = permitting, incentives favoring uninterrupted operation, uneven public disclosure.
- **Cross-impacts:** Flexibility may defer grid upgrades but can shift costs to storage or backup generation; dry cooling can reduce on-site water while raising power demand; location-specific marginal grid emissions and upstream water use matter.
