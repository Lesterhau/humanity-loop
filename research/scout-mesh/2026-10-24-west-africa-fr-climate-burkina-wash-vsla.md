- **Date researched:** 2026-10-24 (America/Chicago)
- **Owner:** Humanity Loop main worker, bounded source review
- **Cell:** `west-africa-fr-climate` — West Africa / French / climate adaptation, water and sanitation
- **Scan decision:** **qualified evidence-to-evaluation opportunity**; no autonomous outreach or new platform. One completed public-source review; **no claim RPC was run**, no Supabase cell timestamps advanced, and autonomous Scout rotation remains unverified under issue #19.
- **Signal class:** emerging implementation variation / potential TIPPO (community finance + community-led sanitation). This is *not* evidence of a new technology or verified climate-health benefit.

## Direct primary observations

1. [UNICEF Burkina Faso, 4 October 2026 (French)](https://www.unicef.org/burkinafaso/recits/le-vsla-et-le-leadership-communautaire-un-duo-efficace-pour-lacc%C3%A8s-%C3%A0-des-services-wash) reports that ASWA III supported community-led total sanitation (ATPC/CLTS) in **67 villages** in the Nando region, and **five** (including Gado) combined ATPC with women's village savings and loan associations (VSLA/AVEC). The article describes an individual using group credit to improve a household latrine and hygiene supplies, and reports community-led support for older residents unable to dig their own pits. **These are programme narratives, not controlled comparative estimates** of sustained latrine access, health gains, loan affordability, disability access, or climate resilience.
2. The same UNICEF article quotes a local leader describing a threatened goat fine for households without latrines and a resident recalling shame at initial triggering. **It does not establish whether a fine was ever imposed**, that this was UNICEF policy, or that particular households were coerced. These are nevertheless implementation-level ethical flags worth checking before replication.
3. [UNICEF ASWA II evaluation summary, 19 March 2026](https://www.unicef.org/evaluation/stories/aswa-ii-how-evaluation-helped-shape-better-future-wash-sub-saharan-africa) reports an earlier multi-country evaluation, including a shift toward climate resilience, inclusive WASH finance, equity measures, and sustainability checks. This shows an **existing evaluation and delivery pathway** rather than an unserved greenfield opportunity. It is not an impact evaluation of the five ASWA III VSLA villages.
4. [WHO/UNICEF 2025 sanitation implementation guidance](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240103009) already provides country-level steps for climate-resilient, safely managed sanitation; [UNICEF disability-inclusive WASH guidance](https://www.unicef.org/documents/guidance-note-disability-inclusive-wash-practices-including-people-disabilities-unicef) covers disability inclusion. [Emergency WASH's CLTS practice framework](https://www.emergency-wash.org/hygiene/en/frameworks/5-community-led-total-sanitation-clts) explicitly warns that shame/disgust can stigmatize vulnerable groups and recommends do-no-harm facilitation. It also describes mixed effectiveness evidence and contextual limits. Do not treat the Gado account as a universal blueprint.

## Novelty and value judgment

**Narrow signal:** linking household savings/credit to sanitation maintenance *may* help bridge the post-triggering finance gap, while strong community leadership can mobilize collective support. The interesting question is whether the combined model outperforms conventional ATPC on **durable, equitable, safe** access without imposing debt or coercive sanctions.

**Duplication check:** UNICEF/ASWA, APS, WHO/UNICEF guidance, and existing CLTS research already address WASH delivery, finance and sustainability. Humanity Loop should **not** build another sanitation app, issue a generic call for funds, or market this as a proven scalable innovation.

**Potential bounded contribution:** identify a publicly available ASWA III evaluation or implementation monitoring dataset that disaggregates the five VSLA villages; if available, create a concise, bilingual evidence-gap and safeguarding crosswalk for an existing implementer to review. If none is public, stop at the documented evidence gap rather than assert performance. Any outreach to implementers should first be de-duplicated, grounded and separately reviewed.

## STEEP+ / TIPPO / horizons

- **STEEP+:** Social—women's leadership, older and disabled residents, stigma; Technological—locally appropriate latrine design, not a digital innovation; Economic—small loans and household affordability; Environmental—water and flood resilience **not measured in this account**; Political/Legal—local authority and threatened sanctions, requiring rights review; Values/Ethical—dignity, consent, equitable access; Demographic—vulnerability and gender; Public health—safe containment, hygiene, maintenance.
- **TIPPO:** Trends—sustainable community WASH; Issues—financing and inclusion gaps; Problems—maintenance affordability and potential coercion; Possibilities—voluntary finance + targeted support for unable-to-pay households; Opportunities—evidence crosswalk using existing programme; no independently substantiated wild-card forecast.
- **Three Horizons:** H1 standard ATPC triggering and household construction; H2 integration with savings groups and local solidarity; H3 rights-preserving, climate-resilient safely managed sanitation with independent long-term outcome verification. H3 is an aspiration, not a measured result.
- **Futures Triangle:** Push—climate stress and sanitation service deficits; Pull—universal dignified safe sanitation; Weight—household costs, debt risk, unequal physical ability, stigma and programme continuity.
- **Cross-impacts:** Credit may improve household materials while burdening low-income families; community sanctions may accelerate uptake while reducing consent/dignity; supporting elderly residents may reduce exclusion; climate shocks may damage latrines and worsen loan repayment. None of these net effects was measured in the cited article.

## Falsification and decision gates

**Support for further action requires:** independent ASWA III results comparing sustained functionality, safely managed waste, household costs/debt, disability inclusion, grievance/consent, and climate-event resilience across VSLA and non-VSLA villages, with selection differences acknowledged. A simpler before/after monitoring report can support learning but not a causal claim.

**Reject scaling claim if:** comparable villages show no improvement in durable access; debt stress or punitive enforcement harms vulnerable households; the model relies on unusually strong leadership not reproducible elsewhere; or an existing evaluator has already fully addressed these questions.

**10th-Man check:** The reported threatened goat fine and shame-triggering could undermine voluntary agency. Their appearance in a story is not proof of an implemented sanction or institutional wrongdoing. Do not amplify the approach without an inclusion/do-no-harm assessment, and credit local implementers for the work already done.
