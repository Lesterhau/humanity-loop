## Distinct actionable distinction

The CDC's **universal U.S.-bound international-flight attestation** is different from the **country- and traveler-status-specific boarding/entry restrictions**. Neither is a blanket claim that every person who has visited Africa is barred from entering the U.S. The rules apply even to certain *airport transit without disembarking*.

| Recent travel history (within previous 21 days) | Traveler category | CDC-described consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DRC, including an airport connection without disembarking | All travelers, including U.S. citizens/nationals | Cannot board a flight to/through the United States within the 21-day interval, absent a separately authorized exemption; CDC says eligible beginning **Day 22** after leaving DRC, if all other requirements met |
| Uganda or South Sudan, but **not** DRC | Non-U.S. citizens/nationals, **including lawful permanent residents** | CDC says U.S. entry restricted until 21 days after leaving; airport transit counts |
| Uganda or South Sudan, but **not** DRC | U.S. citizens/nationals | May enter by air only through designated screening airports (IAD, ATL, JFK) during the 21-day interval |
| No DRC travel in previous 21 days, boarding an international flight into/through U.S. | All travelers, including U.S. citizens and green-card holders | Must complete the DRC absence attestation; airline/aircraft operator collects it |

**Timing precision:** CDC defines **Day 0** as the day of leaving DRC, Day 1 as the first full calendar day outside DRC, and says U.S.-bound boarding can begin on Day 22 if all other requirements are met. CDC's amended attestation order applies to flights departing foreign countries to the U.S. on or after **November 3, 2026 at 11:59 p.m. EST**. It also covers transit through the U.S.; its attestation does **not** apply to land border or seaport entries (other rules may).

**Public-health distinction:** CDC's November 12 situation page says the risk to the U.S. public remains low and no U.S. outbreak-associated cases have been confirmed; this does not mean the outbreak is over. CDC's figures as of **November 10** were **11,145 confirmed cases and 5,437 confirmed deaths** across the listed countries. WHO's **November 6 publication** gives **10,668 DRC cases and 5,198 DRC deaths as of November 3**. These have different **dates and geographic denominators**; comparing the raw totals as a seven-day case increase is invalid. WHO advises against restrictions on international travel/trade in its global outbreak guidance, while U.S. government travel restrictions are separately documented and remain relevant to travelers. Do not present WHO advice as a legal override or infer an official numerical error.

## Unsent public-safe traveler card (requires editorial/legal and public-health review)

**Traveling to or connecting through the United States?** As of the reviewed CDC guidance, everyone boarding an international flight to the U.S. must attest that they have not been in the Democratic Republic of the Congo within the previous 21 days, even if a DRC connection did not involve leaving the aircraft. Recent DRC presence may prevent boarding until Day 22. Separate rules apply to recent Uganda/South Sudan travel and differ for U.S. citizens/nationals versus other travelers, including green-card holders. Before purchasing or changing travel, consult the current CDC [traveler page](https://www.cdc.gov/ebola/situation-summary/returning-travelers.html), the [attestation order](https://www.cdc.gov/port-health/legal-authorities/attestation-order.html), your carrier and appropriate consular/public-health authorities. Do not make a false attestation. If you develop possible Ebola symptoms after relevant travel, isolate and call local health authorities before going to a clinic, as CDC advises.
