## Primary-source evidence

1. **U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission, recall 27-085, dated November 12, 2026:** https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2027/Kakgold-Recalls-4WD-Remote-Controlled-Car-Toys-Due-to-Risk-of-Serious-Injury-or-Death-from-Battery-Ingestion-Violate-Mandatory-Standard-for-Toys
   - About **294** Kakgold 4WD Stunt Vehicle Toys, sold online at Amazon.com **July–September 2026** for **$34–$50**.
   - Product: lime-green/blue vehicle, black chassis, four black wheels with green trim/diagonal rollers, checkered pattern, “X”, “PRO”, “RACING”; hang tag “Do Not Twist the Shaft.”
   - The **remote control** contains accessible button-cell and lithium-ion batteries; risk is potentially fatal battery ingestion, especially for children.
   - **No incidents or injuries reported** as of the recall. This is not evidence of zero risk.
   - CPSC: stop using immediately; contact Kakgold at the **contact shown on the official recall** for full-refund instructions; mark product “RECALLED” and provide a disposal photograph as requested.
   - CPSC **also explicitly warns** that button-cell batteries are hazardous and should follow local hazardous-waste procedures.
2. **U.S. EPA, Used Lithium-Ion Batteries (directly read November 13; page says last updated March 20, 2026):** https://www.epa.gov/recycle/used-lithium-ion-batteries
   - Lithium-ion batteries **and devices containing them should not go into household garbage or ordinary recycling**. Use a suitable electronics/battery recycler or household hazardous-waste program.
   - EPA advises isolating terminals or individually bagging batteries, and states not all batteries are removable or user-serviceable. Damaged batteries require manufacturer guidance.
3. **CPSC, Button Cell and Coin Batteries safety education:** https://www.cpsc.gov/Safety-Education/Safety-Education-Centers/Button-Cell-Coin-Battery-Information-Center
   - Secure loose cells away from children; use nonconductive tape and battery-collection sites. Suspected ingestion warrants **immediate medical attention**. The page lists National Battery Ingestion Hotline **800-498-8666** and Poison Help **800-222-1222**.

## Distinct, useful safety clarification

The recall's refund instructions request a photo of the product being disposed of “in the trash,” **but that is not an instruction to put loose button cells or lithium-ion batteries into household trash**: the same CPSC recall explicitly says batteries need local hazardous-waste handling, and EPA warns against trashing lithium-ion batteries or battery-containing devices. This is an **editorial interpretation of two official instructions**, not a verified defect in the CPSC recall or proof the manufacturer has provided an unsafe individualized disposal instruction. Whether the battery can be safely removed from this exact remote is **not established** by the public recall.

### Human-review-only consumer clarification draft — do not distribute automatically

> **Kakgold 4WD Stunt Vehicle Toys recalled (CPSC 27-085, November 12, 2026).** Stop using the affected toy and keep it and its remote away from children. Check the official CPSC recall's product photos and contact instructions for a full refund. The remote's accessible batteries pose a serious swallowing hazard. Do not put loose button/coin batteries or lithium-ion batteries in household trash or curbside recycling; follow local battery collection or hazardous-waste guidance. If the battery is not safely removable, do not force the remote open: ask the manufacturer or your local waste authority how to handle the battery-containing device while satisfying the recall's refund-photo instructions. If you suspect a battery has been swallowed, seek emergency medical care immediately and contact a poison center. **This is an informational draft, not manufacturer-specific disposal authorization.**
