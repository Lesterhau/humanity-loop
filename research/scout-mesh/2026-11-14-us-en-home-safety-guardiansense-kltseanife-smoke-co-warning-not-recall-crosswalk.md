## Direct primary-source evidence

1. **U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC), Product Safety Warning 27-057, October 29, 2026:** https://www.cpsc.gov/Warnings/2027/CPSC-Warns-Consumers-to-Stop-Using-GuardianSense-KLTseanife-Combination-Smoke-and-Carbon-Monoxide-Detectors-Immediately-Due-to-Risk-of-Serious-Injury-or-Death-from-Fire-Hazard
   - Specific model: **YHW-542COM-R**, sold under **GuardianSense / KLTseanife**, white plastic with digital display; model/manufacturer on rear label. Sold on Amazon/eBay September 2025–June 2026, singly and in multipacks, for $18–$168.
   - CPSC says the units **fail to alarm in hazardous smoke/fire conditions**, with **two reports** of failure to alert in the presence of smoke or fire. This does **not** establish that the carbon-monoxide sensing function was separately tested and found defective.
   - CPSC's action: **stop use immediately, dispose, install working smoke alarms AND CO detectors; do not sell or give away**.
   - CPSC states manufacturer and seller **have not responded to its request to recall**. The publication is a **product safety warning**, **not a completed cooperative product recall**. No official refund or free replacement process is announced in this warning. Do not imply that CPSC has verified a refund entitlement.
   - CPSC specifies alarms meeting the relevant **UL 217 (smoke) and UL 2034 (CO)** standards, installed on each level and outside separate sleeping areas, with battery or battery backup.
2. **U.S. Fire Administration, Smoke Alarms (page reviewed October 13, 2026):** https://www.usfa.fema.gov/prevention/home-fires/prepare-for-fire/smoke-alarms/
   - Smoke alarms inside every bedroom, outside each separate sleeping area, on every level including basement; interconnected where possible; follow local requirements and installation instructions.
   - Test functioning alarms monthly and replace old alarms on the recommended schedule; some local fire departments provide no-cost smoke-alarm installation.
3. **CDC, Carbon Monoxide Poisoning Basics (updated October 26, 2026):** https://www.cdc.gov/carbon-monoxide/about/
   - Install battery-operated/battery-backup CO detectors near sleeping areas; follow replacement and maintenance instructions. CO is odorless and dangerous.
   - CDC's CO health and placement guidance is general, not a separate technical test of this specific product.

## Distinct, actionable safety-information clarification

**Avoid three dangerous conflations:**
- **Warning ≠ completed recall or verified refund.** CPSC reports an unresponsive manufacturer/seller; a consumer should not wait for a manufacturer-funded replacement before restoring working alarms.
- **Smoke-sensing failure ≠ proven CO-sensing failure.** The product is sold as a combination alarm, but CPSC's cited failure is smoke/fire detection. Its replacement recommendation explicitly calls for **both** smoke and CO coverage.
- **A reassuring button beep does not cancel the CPSC stop-use instruction.** Monthly testing is appropriate for compliant replacement alarms, but consumers should not use a self-test as justification to keep using this specifically warned-about model. This last sentence is a conservative interpretation of CPSC's unconditional stop-use direction, not a separate published CPSC laboratory finding about test-button behavior.
