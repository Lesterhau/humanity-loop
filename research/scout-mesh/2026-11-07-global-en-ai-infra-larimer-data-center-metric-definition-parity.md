## Primary evidence, inspected November 7, 2026 UTC

1. [Larimer County's official draft-regulations overview](https://www.larimer.gov/draft-data-center-regulations) describes the unincorporated-county process, October 26–November 24 public-feedback window, and December 16/January 11 future hearings.
2. [Larimer County's public Engage Portal](https://engage.larimer.gov/x00418), **Water and Wastewater** section and repeated FAQ, specifies a proposed WUE standard **0.8**, describes WUE as water used divided by power consumed, then says **“The higher the ratio, the more water-efficient”**. The same page says the draft calls for **at least 80% renewable energy**.
3. [Actual 27-page draft Land Use Code amendment](https://publicinput.com/Customer/File/Full/87e4204c-1731-4758-b9cc-a6eac75d3fad), §17.3.4(A)(2), page 6 of PDF (printed page 6): “A Water Use Efficiency (WUE) standard of 0.8 is required”; denominator is described as **total power consumed**, numerator as **direct plus indirect water used for power generation**. The text does **not** specify whether 0.8 is an upper bound, units, averaging period, or the measurement method for indirect water.
4. The same draft, §17.3.4(C)(2), printed page 7, says **REF of 0.8** is required but defines REF as **renewable energy / non-renewable energy**. The public overview describes an **80% renewable share** and hourly matching.
5. [U.S. DOE Federal Energy Management Program, Cooling Water Efficiency Opportunities for Federal Data Centers](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/femp/cooling-water-efficiency-opportunities-federal-data-centers), January 9, 2019, defines *site* WUE as **annual site water use (liters) / annual IT-equipment energy (kWh)**. At fixed IT energy, a **lower**, not higher, WUE means less site water use. DOE's site metric differs from Larimer's proposed direct+indirect water and total-energy boundary, so the numeric **0.8 cannot be directly compared without clarifying scope**.
6. [European Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2024/1364, Annex III](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_del/2024/1364/oj/eng), published May 17, 2024, independently defines WUE as water input / IT energy and REF as renewable energy / total data-center energy. It is **technical comparator evidence, not law applicable to Larimer County**. [ISO/IEC 30134-3:2016](https://www.iso.org/standard/66127.html) also identifies REF as a standard KPI; its full text was not purchased.

## Specific inconsistencies and mathematical check

**A. Inverted public WUE explanation:** If WUE = water / energy, at equal energy, more water yields a larger ratio. Thus the Engage Portal's statement that *higher WUE is more water-efficient* is inverted. Its wording is repeated in the FAQ. This is a demonstrable educational error independent of whether the county adopts 0.8.

**B. Ambiguous proposed WUE standard:** The draft uses direct **and indirect** water / **total** power; DOE's standard *site* WUE uses site water / IT energy. Larimer may deliberately define a broader metric, but the draft does not specify liters/kWh, upper-bound direction, or precise measurement and verification boundaries. The correct treatment of 0.8 requires expert confirmation, not unilateral substitution.

**C. REF denominator contradicts the stated 80% goal:** For renewable energy R and nonrenewable N, the draft's literal REF = R/N. If REF = 0.8, the renewable share of total is R/(R+N) = 0.8/1.8 = **44.44%**, not 80%. An 80% renewable share requires R/(R+N) = 0.8, equivalent to R/N = **4** (when N > 0). The draft's REF definition also differs from the standard renewable/total formulation in the EU's Annex III. The county's policy intent appears to be 80% renewables, but legal interpretation and enforcement consequences are **not established**.

## Human-review-only neutral clarification draft (NOT sent)

> During review of the proposed data-center Land Use Code amendment, could staff clarify two technical metric definitions? The Engage Portal describes a higher water-usage-effectiveness ratio as more efficient, whereas water divided by energy decreases when less water is used at fixed energy. The draft's §17.3.4(A)(2) also does not state whether 0.8 is a maximum, its units, or how direct and indirect water and total power are measured. Separately, §17.3.4(C)(2) defines REF as renewable energy divided by nonrenewable energy and requires 0.8, whereas the public summary describes at least 80% renewable energy; 0.8 on that stated denominator equals about 44.4% of total energy. Could the final text specify the intended numerator, denominator, compliance direction, units, reporting period, and verification method for both metrics? We are seeking technical clarification, not alleging that any project is noncompliant.
