## Primary-source review and scope

1. **UNESCO, 1 April 2026 — 2026 Global Education Monitoring regional analysis:** UNESCO reports **273 million** children and youth out of school **worldwide** and says three quarters of out-of-school children/youth are in **Central and Southern Asia or sub-Saharan Africa combined**; the figure must **not** be described as South Asia alone. It also reports that **17% of countries in Central and Southern Asia** still legally require segregation of children with disabilities in separate settings; this is a regional-country proportion, not a count of students, and does not identify an uncorrected specific country law. The same source documents inclusive-education policies and redistribution measures already operating. Source: https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/south-asia-has-high-out-school-rates-yet-learners-who-enter-school-continue-through-and-succeed?hub=171169 (published 2026-04-01).
2. **NITI Aayog, May 2026 — `School Education System in India`:** Figure 3.43 cites **ASER 2024**, not a fresh 2026 survey: household smartphone access among ages 14–16 was **90.2% boys / 88.1% girls**, but only **70.2% boys / 62.2% girls** brought a usable connected phone to the enumerator's digital-task exercise; personal ownership was **36.2% / 26.9%** among those able to operate a phone. This suggests a gap between nominal household access and practical device control, especially for girls; it does **not** prove an individual service denied anyone education. NITI explicitly says its publication is independent research, **not a formal Government of India policy statement**. Source: https://www.niti.gov.in/node/2288 (report dated May 2026; underlying ASER 2024 data).
3. **UNESCO, 2025 — Digital transformation in South Asian higher education:** Its six-country review (Bhutan, India, Maldives, Nepal, Pakistan, Sri Lanka) documents uneven institutional infrastructure, skills and policy alignment, while identifying existing university partnerships and capacity-building programs. Higher education findings cannot be directly generalized to 14–16-year-old schoolchildren. Source: https://www.unesco.org/sdg4education2030/en/publication/report-digital-transformation-higher-education-south-asia (2025).
4. **Existing public solution — NCERT/Ministry of Education DIKSHA and PM e-VIDYA:** DIKSHA already provides curriculum materials and multilingual, accessible learning pathways; official PM e-VIDYA describes television/radio/offline and special-needs alternatives, including content for hearing/visually impaired learners. Availability of these services **does not prove equitable real-world uptake, content quality, or accessibility for all users**. Sources: https://diksha.gov.in/about/ ; https://pmevidya.education.gov.in/ ; https://pmevidya.education.gov.in/cwsn.html (live pages accessed 2026-10-26).
