## Direct primary-source evidence

1. EPA's current permit landing page, last updated **October 15, 2026**, says public comments are being accepted **through November 16, 2026** for draft NPDES permit **MT-0000007**. It links a draft permit and Statement of Basis both labeled **October 8, 2026**.  
   https://www.epa.gov/npdes-permits/npdes-permit-chippewa-cree-no-winz-ro-treatment-plant-montana
2. The EPA public-notice page for the **same permit** instead displays **“Publish Date March 6, 2026”** and says the public-comment period ends **30 calendar days after publication**. Calculated literally from March 6, that is **April 5, 2026**, not November 16. The page is also marked last updated October 15, 2026 and links the October 8 draft documents. It does not explain a re-notice or extension in the displayed text.  
   https://www.epa.gov/npdes-permits/opportunity-comment-draft-permit-chippewa-cree-no-winz-reverse-osmosis-treatment
3. EPA draft permit, pp. 1, 8–9, identifies the proposed discharge to a tributary of Big Sandy Creek, sets TSS limits of **29 mg/L 30-day average** and **65 mg/L daily maximum**, and pH **6.5–9.0**. **Arsenic, nickel, thallium and TDS are report-only**, with **annual** sampling for those constituents; flow, TSS and pH are sampled at every discharge. The draft's effective/expiration dates are still “to be determined upon issuance.” This is a **draft**, not evidence of an issued authorization or current discharge.  
   https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2026-10/mt0000007-rocky-boys-ro-draft-npdes-permit-2026.pdf
4. EPA Statement of Basis, pp. 10–11, says source reject-water measurements detected total arsenic **12 µg/L**, nickel **7 µg/L**, and thallium **1.4 µg/L**. EPA discusses separate fish-consumption human-health criteria (arsenic **0.14 µg/L**, thallium **0.47 µg/L**) but proposes additional monitoring for a future reasonable-potential analysis, rather than numeric effluent limits for those metals now. These values are **not directly interchangeable** with a downstream exposure or drinking-water test: total vs dissolved fractions, mixing/dilution, sampling timing, water use and permit law need qualified review. The Statement of Basis expects intermittent discharge approximately every three months and says Big Sandy Creek/Milk River are not presently used as drinking water on the Reservation.  
   https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2026-10/mt0000007-rocky-boys-ro-draft-sob-2026.pdf

## Verified narrow finding

The **two EPA webpages conflict on how to calculate the public-comment period**. The main permit page explicitly advertises November 16; the linked notice's March 6 publication metadata and 30-day formula imply April 5. It is possible that the notice metadata is stale, that the permit was re-noticed, or that an extension applies. **The controlling procedural history and acceptance cutoff are not independently verified.** Do not tell a member of the public that the period legally closed in April or that a late submission will be accepted.

The separate metals/monitoring information raises a **legitimate, neutral technical-review question**, not proof that EPA, the Tribe or the plant violated a standard or caused harm. The Tribal Water Resources Department is the proposed permittee; do not imply lack of Tribal agency, consent or consultation.
