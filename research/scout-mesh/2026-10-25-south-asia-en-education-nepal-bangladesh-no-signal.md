## Evidence reviewed (dates and scope matter)

1. **Nepal foundational learning (13 October 2026):** UNESCO Institute for Statistics reports Nepal's Education Review Office piloted AMPL reading/mathematics assessment material in **37 schools across 12 districts**, with a main administration planned for **March 2027**. Observers in two schools found **44 of 50** sampled pupils participated; these are process observations, **not** learning-outcome results or proof of a national assessment defect. Nepal ERO, UIS, UNESCO Kathmandu and the World Bank already own this work. Primary source: https://www.uis.unesco.org/en/news/nepal-prepares-strengthen-evidence-foundational-learning
2. **Nepal flood learning continuity (UNICEF, 23–24 September 2026):** UNICEF's dated initial assessment said **more than 14,000 children** experienced learning disruption across **62 schools/learning centres** in Dhading, Nuwakot and Rasuwa; UNICEF supported **48 temporary learning centres** helping **more than 1,400 children** return to learning by that report date. These are **dated response figures**, not current October totals. Primary source: https://www.unicef.org/nepal/press-releases/one-month-after-floods-efforts-restore-education-are-helping-children-return
3. **Nepal follow-through (17 October 2026):** The Kathmandu Post, attributing data to the Ministry of Education and Sports and National Planning Commission, reports **56 temporary learning centres** established, **57** institutions assessed, **28** physically damaged, and **only 10 of 57** assessed institutions with disaster-risk-reduction plans. The government has a five-year recovery strategy and intends to monitor displaced pupils. **Do not mix the 17 October assessment denominator with UNICEF's September 62-school preliminary scope**, or infer that every other school is presently unsafe. Secondary, government-attributed report: https://kathmandupost.com/national/2026/10/17/bhotekoshi-flood-displaced-777-students-nepal-plans-five-year-school-recovery
4. **Bangladesh Rohingya education (UNICEF report for 1 January–30 June 2026):** UNICEF and Save the Children co-led an education response serving **302,800 learners**; UNICEF reported **more than 150,000** Rohingya children aged 5–18 outside *Education Sector-supported* learning opportunities (some may attend other provision; **not** proof that all receive no education). UNICEF identified **1,397 learning facilities without repair funding**, of which **300** needed full reconstruction, in that **June snapshot**; the report also recorded a **US$3.7 million** gap in its highest-priority education interventions. No evidence reviewed establishes those exact gaps remain unchanged in October. Primary source: https://www.unicef.org/media/183486/file/Bangladesh-Humanitarian-Situation-Report-No.75%28Mid-Year%29%2C30-June-2026.pdf.pdf (pp. 2, 4–5).
5. **Existing response and appeal (20 May and 20 October 2026):** The UN's **US$710.5 million** 2026 Rohingya appeal explicitly includes **US$52.7 million for education** and named humanitarian partners; UNICEF's October 20 statement renewed the request for education support, including Rohingya children. These are established funding/coordination channels, not a new unserved funder match. Sources: https://bangladesh.un.org/en/315903-un-and-partners-appeal-usd-7105-million-meet-critical-needs-rohingya-refugees-and ; https://www.unicef.org/press-releases/unicef-executive-director-calls-urgent-investment-children-bangladesh-and-measures
