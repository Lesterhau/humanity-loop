## Bounded question and primary-source observations

Could Humanity Loop make a distinctive, immediate, safe contribution to reducing the grid/ratepayer burden of new AI data centers, rather than duplicating active official and research programs?

1. **Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, June 2026:** *Speed to Power: Solutions for Accelerating Large Load Connections* already identifies **more than 40 potential solutions** across forecasting, interconnection, procurement/planning, markets/operations, and cost allocation/ratemaking. It describes a solution landscape, not independently verified successful deployment of each option. Source: https://eta.lbl.gov/publications/speed-power-solutions-accelerating
2. **LBNL / National Academy of Engineering, May 2026:** *Integrating AI Data Centers with the Power Grid* describes **four** flexibility mechanisms: shifting computation, flexible facility operation, energy storage, and onsite generation. It notes tariff and interconnection incentives, and expressly cautions that flexibility cannot replace long-term new generation. The article does not prove any particular site's flexibility or net community benefit. Source: https://eta-publications.lbl.gov/publications/integrating-ai-data-centers-power
3. **LBNL, September 2026:** Its *2026 Large Load Literature Review and Data Sources* already catalogs **41 publications** in 12 categories, including demand flexibility, rate impacts, interconnection and reliability. A generic new directory or summary would be duplicative absent a verified user-specific gap. Source: https://energyanalysis.lbl.gov/publications/2026-large-load-literature-review
4. **U.S. Department of Energy, June 18, 2026:** DOE reports that FERC directed **six regional grid operators** to justify or reform large-load interconnection tariffs under Federal Power Act section 206. This is a reported regulatory action and stated ratepayer-protection objective, **not proof** that cost shifting has ceased or that the reforms have been implemented uniformly. Source: https://www.energy.gov/articles/department-energy-applauds-fercs-action-large-load-interconnection-reform
