2. [COFEPRIS, 21 October 2026](https://www.gob.mx/cofepris/es/articulos/mexico-avanza-en-estrategia-nacional-para-prevenir-y-controlar-la-exposicion-al-plomo?idiom=es) announces a **proposed** four-standard update, including a planned paint lead limit of 90 ppm, ceramics restrictions, and a proposed no-cost product certification scheme. An announcement is **not** proof that all standards or certification services are implemented.

[Mexican official gazette, 5 January 2026](https://www.dof.gob.mx/nota_detalle.php?codigo=5777846&fecha=05/01/2026) identifies `PROY-NOM-003-SSA1-2025` as a **draft submitted for consultation**, not an enacted standard. The official January consultation window is over; do not invite late comments as if still open.
4. [Mexican official gazette, 27 January 2020](https://dof.gob.mx/nota_detalle.php?codigo=5584840&fecha=27/01/2020) contains an earlier **PROY-NOM-003-SSA1-2018 draft** mentioning 90 ppm. Its explicit *PROYECTO* status matters: this is not evidence that the 90 ppm rule was already law in 2020. The [2008 official NOM-003-SSA1-2006](https://dof.gob.mx/nota_detalle.php?codigo=5055685&fecha=04/08/2008) and [IPEN's 2020 Mexico paint study](https://ipen.org/sites/default/files/documents/ipen-lead-playgrounds-mexico_v1_2-es-web.pdf) provide context for the older 600 ppm labeling distinction. Legal-currentness requires checking any later final publication before asserting what is in force.

[Pure Earth, June 2025](https://www.pureearth.org/pure-earth-mexico-participates-and-promotes-lead-free-pottery-at-the-2025-diplomatic-cultural-bazaar/) describes an **existing** lead-free pottery and artisan market-access program; [PAHO, November 2025](https://www.paho.org/es/noticias/26-11-2025-ops-llama-avanzar-prevencion-vigilancia-contaminacion-por-exposicion-al-plomo) describes cross-agency efforts with Pure Earth and Vital Strategies. Duplicative standalone tooling or unsolicited outreach is not warranted on these facts.
