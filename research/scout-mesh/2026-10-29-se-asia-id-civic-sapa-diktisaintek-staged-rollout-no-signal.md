## Source-reviewed observations

1. **Ministry's own launch release, 28 October 2026:** The ministry says SAPA is a single-entry service portal across **six service segments** and **48 mapped services**, with **11 priority services prepared in the initial phase**. It describes service standards, requirements, process steps, responsible units and application tracking. It expressly says procedures, response times, assistance/complaint channels, security and privacy will be improved in stages. The release does **not** establish that all 48 services were live on launch day. Source: https://www.kemdiktisaintek.go.id/news/article/integrasikan-layanan-kemdiktisaintek-hadirkan-sapa-diktisaintek (published 2026-10-28).
2. **Public university confirmation, 28 October:** Universitas Nusa Cendana reports that the ministry mapped 48 services and made 11 priority services available initially. It identifies early higher-education, lecturer/staff and public-facing services, including recognition of prior learning, lecturer certification, academic-rank processes and foreign-degree equivalence. This is institutional reporting of the launch, **not** independent testing of each workflow or evidence of service-time savings. Source: https://undana.ac.id/2026/pangkas-birokrasi-kemdiktisaintek-resmi-luncurkan-portal-terpadu-sapa-diktisaintek/ .
3. **Separate public university account, 29 October:** Universitas Sultan Ageng Tirtayasa likewise describes 48 mapped versus 11 initial priority services and says complaint channels, procedures and privacy protections are part of the continuing improvement program. Source: https://untirta.ac.id/2026/10/29/kemdiktisaintek-luncurkan-sapa-diktisaintek-perkuat-transformasi-layanan-publik-yang-transparan-dan-adaptif/ .
4. **Public portal reachability limitation:** A generic web document reader could not fetch `https://sapa.kemdiktisaintek.go.id/` during this review. ## Novelty, ownership, alternatives and uncertainty

**Observed signal:** A national ministry is consolidating higher-education administrative service discovery and status tracking, potentially reducing the need to navigate multiple institutional sites. This is a documented transition, **not a demonstrated improvement in access or processing time**.

**Existing owner and solutions:** The ministry's public launch and Pusdatin organization explicitly own the rollout and acknowledge staged improvements. The initial **11-of-48** distinction is already published; claiming the remaining 37 are an undisclosed outage would be misleading. A separate, locally reported instruction to move services fully online does not itself establish that offline accommodations have been removed or that users were denied service.

**Unverified:** whether the 11 workflows actually complete; accessible mobile/screen-reader behavior; availability of human assistance; tracking accuracy; complaint response time; security/privacy implementation; whether every segment is populated. These are potential evaluation questions, not proven defects.
