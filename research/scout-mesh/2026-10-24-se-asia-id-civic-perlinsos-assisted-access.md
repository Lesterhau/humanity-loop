## Evidence (Indonesian primary institutional sources, checked 24 October 2026)
1. **Ministry of Communication and Digital, 11 October 2026:** The Bali Perlinsos pilot had 652,858 registered households (50.52% of Bali households) in the first week of October. The ministry reports **93.35% of registered households were assisted by Perlinsos agents**, with 12,187 active agents, despite reporting 80.88% internet access among Bali residents. The internet statistic and registered-household statistic have different denominators; neither independently proves that 93.35% could not self-register. [Komdigi report](https://djtpd.komdigi.go.id/berita/pelajaran-dari-bali-mira-tayyiba-sebut-gotong-royong-kunci-digitalisasi-bansos).
2. **Indonesian Presidency, 20 October 2026:** Officials reported 11.5 million households registered nationally across all 514 districts/cities in a pilot, supported by approximately 775,000 agents; a national launch was *planned* for 10 November 2026. These are attributed government figures and a stated plan, not independently audited outcomes or proof that launch has occurred. [Presidential report](https://presidenri.go.id/siaran-pers/bansos-lebih-adil-dan-tepat-sasaran-presiden-prabowo-terima-laporan-perlinsos-digital-115-juta-kk-terdaftar/).
3. **Kementerian Agama, Barito Selatan, 5 October 2026:** Officials explicitly describe two registration paths: independent access via digital identity (IKD), and assisted registration through Agen Perlinsos, including support for older residents, disabled residents and people facing biometric-verification difficulties. [Official account](https://barsel.kemenag.go.id/berita/portal-perlinsos-disiapkan-untuk-bansos-2027-kakankemenag-barsel-ikuti-sosialisasi-via-zoom).
4. **Gunungkidul social-services department, accessed 24 October 2026:** Its public Perlinsos guidance explicitly states people without smartphones can register through official agents/staff. [Official service guidance](https://sosial.gunungkidulkab.go.id/).
5. **Kulon Progo social-services department, 24 September 2026:** Local agency reported 255 agents and 1,832 registered households (1.15% of its 159,770 households) at that time; its notice describes in-person help for older, disabled and phone-less residents. This is a historical locality-specific figure, **not** a verified current coverage rate or a national benchmark. [Local government report](https://pemkab.kulonprogokab.go.id/publikasi/detail/percepat-perlinsos-digital-dinsos-pppa-kulon-progo-gembleng-pendamping-sosial).
6. **Ombudsman RI, 15 September 2026:** A public commentary cautions that digital-only public services can exclude older, rural and low-income residents, and urges accessible alternatives and measurement of failed applications. This is a general institutional warning, **not** a Perlinsos-specific finding of wrongdoing. [Ombudsman commentary](https://www.ombudsman.go.id/artikel/r/artikel--layanan-pindah-ke-ponsel-siapa-yang-ditinggalkan).

## Interpretation and counterevidence
**Signal:** A supposedly digital-first welfare transition is, at least in the Bali pilot, predominantly **human-assisted digital access**. For this particular program, implementation and monitoring may depend more on agent reach, accessibility, safe data handling and the quality of completed registrations than on portal traffic alone.

**Existing solution / duplication:** The government has already deployed agent-assisted enrollment, local face-to-face support, data challenge routes, and national expansion plans. An independent sign-up portal, duplicate field survey or claim that the system is digital-only is unjustified. The official high assisted-registration share is not itself proof of a defect; it could reflect deliberate outreach, program design or population mix.

**STEEP+:** Social/demographic—access for older, rural, low-income and disabled people; Technological—IKD/biometric registration plus agent interface; Economic—potential avoided travel and exclusion costs, not quantified; Political/legal—public service and data-protection duties; Values/ethical—consent, dignity and practical choice; Environmental—no supported finding.

**TIPPO:** Trend—rapid nationwide welfare digitalization; Issue—potential agent-dependency and uneven service completion; Plan—government agent network and November launch; Projection—agent quality may dominate inclusion (hypothesis); Obstacle—public reports emphasize registrations, not independently measured completed, equitable service access.

**Three Horizons:** H1 fragmented/manual welfare administration; H2 digital enrollment with human-assisted access; H3 accessible-by-default services with auditable support and appeals.

**Futures Triangle:** Push—national data integration; Pull—fairer welfare access; Weight—device, identity, disability and local staffing constraints.

**Cross-impact:** Faster enrollment may reduce exclusion from obsolete records, but insufficient agent support or weak privacy controls could disadvantage vulnerable residents. No such specific harm is established here.

**Heuristic scores (0–1; judgment, not measurements):** novelty 0.47, usefulness 0.79, evidence quality 0.85 for the quoted *official reports* (lower for actual causal outcomes), interpretation uncertainty 0.55.

**10th-Man:** Government and local agents already recognize and address the problem; external intervention could distract or duplicate their work. A 93.35% assisted share can be a success metric, not a failure metric. Do not treat low registration in one September locality as a national access failure.

## Falsification / evidence needed
- Publish or independently validate agent-assisted versus self-service **completion**, successful benefit eligibility review, appeal outcomes, failures/abandonment, wait time and geographic/disability/age coverage, with privacy-preserving aggregation.
- Determine whether the 93.35% share changes with adoption and whether assisted registrants experience materially different completion/error rates.
- A documented nationwide agent support and grievance system with measured equitable outcomes would substantially weaken any unmet-access hypothesis.
- Do not collect beneficiary PII or run unsanctioned accessibility/biometric tests.
