## Evidence: official Spanish-language sources

1. [UNICEF Panamá, emergency response page, **updated 19 October 2026**](https://www.unicef.org/panama/emergencia_terremoto_panama) reports that the **9 October** earthquake left an estimated **38,000 people, including 8,300 children**, needing humanitarian assistance. Its **16 October** situation figures include **619 affected schools**, **35 severely damaged/collapsed**, and **3,696 people in more than 46 temporary shelters**. These are date-stamped estimates, not verified current-day counts.
2. [UNICEF Latin America press release, **13 October 2026**](https://www.unicef.org/lac/comunicados-prensa/mas-de-8000-ninos-y-ninas-necesitan-asistencia-humanitaria-tras-terremoto) used an earlier preliminary figure of **656 damaged schools**, described at least **25 health facilities** affected, and reported **16 tents made available** to health authorities. The difference between 656 (earlier release) and 619 (later situation page) is **not evidence of 37 repairs**: methods, inspection coverage, and definitions are not reconciled in these two sources.
3. UNICEF's updated Panama page reports **415 hygiene kits distributed on Isla Cañas**, and tents delivered to health facilities to support continuity of care. It describes plans for additional supplies and WASH rehabilitation. A plan, procurement, deployment, distribution and restored service are **different milestones**.
4. [PAHO/OPS, **20 July 2026**, regional disability inclusion report](https://www.paho.org/es/noticias/20-7-2026-inclusion-personas-con-discapacidad-sistemas-salud-gestion-emergencias-sigue) describes persistent disability-access gaps in emergency health systems across ten Latin American/Caribbean countries. This is a **regional risk lens**, not proof of a specific disability-related failure in Panama's current response.

### Ficha breve en español

**Necesidad confirmada:** UNICEF Panamá estima que 8.300 niñas, niños y adolescentes requieren asistencia después del sismo del 9 de octubre. Su plan incluye agua segura, continuidad de la atención sanitaria, apoyo psicosocial y rehabilitación de servicios. **No confundir metas con entregas:** los 14.150 beneficiarios previstos para agua temporal y los 20.000 previstos para recuperación de sistemas no son resultados ejecutados. La página sí comunica la distribución de 415 kits de higiene en Isla Cañas. La cifra de 619 escuelas afectadas tiene un corte distinto al de las 656 del comunicado anterior; no atribuir la diferencia a reparaciones sin evidencia.

**Posible contribución limitada:** comprobar con los responsables de la respuesta, mediante canales autorizados y revisión humana previa, si existe una necesidad de insumos específica y todavía no cubierta antes de proponer una conexión con proveedores o donantes. ## Novelty, duplication, and foresight

- **Existing solutions checked:** government-led disaster response; UNICEF Panamá's active emergency programme and direct fundraising/corporate-partnership pathway; regional PAHO/OPS disability-inclusive emergency-health guidance. A separate Humanity Loop delivery service or generic donation campaign would duplicate established channels.
- **STEEP+:** Social—displacement and child safeguarding; Technological—portable water-treatment and temporary clinical facilities; Economic—recovery finance and supply bottlenecks; Environmental—water-system damage; Political/Legal—government-led coordination, procurement, safeguarding; Values/Ethical—disability inclusion, dignity, consent and transparent claims; Demographic—children, caregivers and rural communities.
- **TIPPOs:** Trend—climate/disaster continuity planning; Issue—essential-service disruption; Plan—UNICEF sector-specific reach targets; Projection—none independently validated beyond published target; Obstacle—unverified delivery rates, infrastructure access and potential supply constraints.
- **Three Horizons:** H1 emergency tents/water deliveries; H2 repair of essential water and health systems with accessible temporary services; H3 resilient, inclusive services designed for future shocks (aspiration, not an achieved result).
- **Futures Triangle:** Push—acute disruption and displaced families; Pull—reliable child-safe access to water, healthcare and school; Weight—damaged infrastructure, logistics and pre-existing inequality.
- **Cross-impacts:** water-system failure can affect infection prevention and clinic function; school closure can increase psychosocial needs; temporary service provision can preserve care but create dependence if rehabilitation lags. These are plausible pathways, not quantified causal effects in reviewed sources.
