# Bambora sling carrier: CPSC November 12 safety warning is not an announced refund recall

**Observed:** 2026-11-14 UTC. **Scope:** U.S. / English / infant consumer-product safety; one bounded, primary-source Tier-0 research check. **Status:** public-source evidence and UNSENT caregiver-information draft for qualified human review. This is not agency outreach, outside publication, an independently accepted contributor submission, a formal Supabase Scout completion, or a verified prevented injury.

## Primary evidence and direct observations

1. [U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission, Product Safety Warning 27-079, dated November 12, 2026](https://www.cpsc.gov/Warnings/2027/CPSC-Warns-Consumers-to-Stop-Using-Bambora-Sling-Carriers-Immediately-Due-to-Risk-of-Serious-Injury-or-Death-from-Fall-Hazard-Violate-Mandatory-Standard-for-Sling-Carriers), directly inspected November 14. CPSC identifies Bambora Sling Carriers with several prints, an adjustable **black strap**, and a stitched label at the strap end reading **“Bambora” and “Bambora Sling Carrier.”** CPSC states that the restraint strap can fail and separate, posing a potentially fatal **infant fall** hazard. The company was issued a Notice of Violation and had **not responded** as reported in the warning. CPSC reports **no incidents or injuries** for this warning. The agency identifies online sales at Bamboraco.com, prices $59–$118. No number of affected units is given.
2. [CPSC's federal sling-carrier standard FAQ](https://www.cpsc.gov/FAQ/Sling-Carriers), accessed November 14. CPSC explains that the mandatory regulation addresses infant falls, structural failures and safe-use education; it cites 16 CFR part 1228. This provides background on the standard, not a second independent Bambora failure report.
3. [CPSC's February 1, 2018 sling standard notice](https://www.cpsc.gov/content/new-federal-standard-to-improve-safety-of-infant-slings-takes-effect), accessed November 14. General safe positioning and hardware-check advice applies to other compliant carriers; it **cannot** make a structurally unsafe Bambora sling safe to continue using.

## Verified distinction and potential public value

**Warning versus recall:** CPSC's November 12 item is explicitly a **Product Safety Warning**, not a completed cooperative manufacturer recall. It provides **no established refund, replacement, or repair program**. Do not tell parents that a refund is available, or assume a seller has acknowledged the defect. No reported injuries is not proof that a product is safe.

**Specific failure versus general infant-sling risks:** The Bambora notice specifically identifies **restraint strap separation and infant falls**. Other CPSC materials discuss suffocation hazards in some sling use, but the November 12 Bambora notice does **not** independently document a Bambora suffocation defect or death. Do not imply all infant slings are defective.

**Agency action:** CPSC urges consumers to **stop using Bambora slings immediately**, cut the fabric in half to render them unusable, dispose of them, and **not sell or give them away**. A parent should first arrange an alternative safe way to carry the infant; routine visual inspection or tightening the strap is **not** a CPSC-authorized workaround. Exact product identification should use the label and CPSC photographs, not just a vaguely similar print.
