## Direct primary-source findings (and temporal boundaries)

1. **AGRHYMET CCR-AOS, technical note no. 4, published 23 October 2026, original French, pp. 1, 3–7.** Its September-initialized seasonal forecast for **October–December 2026 (OND)** anticipates below-average rainfall in parts of Senegal, Guinea, Sierra Leone and Liberia; near-to-above-average totals in southern Mali, Burkina Faso, northern Côte d'Ivoire, Ghana, Togo and Benin; and a wetter signal in parts of central/northern Nigeria. For **November 2026–January 2027 (NDJ)** it expects rain to concentrate toward the Gulf of Guinea, with below-average rain in parts of Ghana, Togo, Benin and especially southern Nigeria. Above-normal temperatures are forecast over much of the region during OND. These are *seasonal probabilistic spatial patterns*, not village-level or day-specific alerts. The note explicitly cautions about localized flooding alongside water/heat stress. [Original AGRHYMET PDF](https://agrhymet.cilss.int/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Note_technique_AGRHYMET_MAJ-prevision_octobre2026.pdf) | [publication page](https://agrhymet.cilss.int/2026/10/23/note-technique-n4-agrhymet-ccr-aos-retrait-de-la-mousson-au-sahel-et-concentration-des-pluies-vers-le-golfe-de-guinee-sur-fond-de-fort-el-nino/).
2. **FEWS NET, West Africa Seasonal Monitor, 29 September 2026.** Reports accumulated rainfall deficits and reduced vegetation/crop water availability in localized northern Sahel zones; long dry spells in Senegal, Mauritania and Mali; and **above-normal rainfall forecast for October–November across the Sahel**, with late rains unlikely to fully reverse earlier deficits. Its model-based low major-river flood likelihood refers to the **remainder of that season**, not a guarantee against localized flash floods. [FEWS NET original monitor](https://fews.net/west-africa/seasonal-monitor/september-2026).
3. **UNDP, French-language West/Central Africa briefing, 14 September 2026.** Explicitly notes disagreement among regional rainfall models, with conflict-driven vulnerability and funding constraints underlying the risk; calls for conflict-sensitive anticipatory action and country-level interpretation rather than treating El Niño as a uniform hazard. The 52.8 million figure is a **June–August 2026 projected food-insecurity burden**, not a newly observed October 2026 count. [UNDP original briefing](https://www.undp.org/fr/africa/waca/communiques/le-pnud-appelle-un-investissement-anticipatoire-urgent-alors-quel-nino-vient-aggraver-une-crise-deja-tiree-par-les-conflits-0).
4. **AGRHYMET, 13 October 2026.** Its WISER CLICK/ACCORD workshop already addresses the translation of climate information into agriculture/health decisions, delivery channels, local context and uncertainty. This is an existing solution pathway, not evidence that every last-mile delivery problem is solved. [AGRHYMET service co-production report](https://agrhymet.cilss.int/2026/10/13/services-climatiques-agrhymet-renforce-le-passage-de-la-prevision-climatique-a-la-decision/).

## Independent cross-check and non-duplication decision

The FEWS NET **September-issued October–November Sahel-wide** forecast and AGRHYMET's later **October–December subregional** forecast cannot be treated as directly contradictory without matching issue dates, geographies, models, seasonal windows and confidence. AGRHYMET's western dry / central-eastern wetter split is a useful refinement, while UNDP had already explicitly warned of model divergence. In particular, a major-river flood outlook is not a local flash-flood warning; a wetter regional seasonal outlook is not proof that earlier crop deficits were reversed. The sources reviewed do **not** show a concrete official public error, missing warning delivery, unserved community, or a novel solution Humanity Loop could responsibly implement now.

**Existing response:** AGRHYMET already publishes regional and national-service forecasts and works with partners on last-mile decision translation; FEWS NET monitors food-security climate conditions; UNDP explicitly promotes anticipatory planning. Publishing another generic alarm, contacting agencies without a specific defect, or building a duplicate forecast app would have poor marginal value.
