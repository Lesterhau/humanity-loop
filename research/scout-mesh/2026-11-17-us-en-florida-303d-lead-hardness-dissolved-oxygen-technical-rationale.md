# Florida 2024 303(d): technical rationale behind EPA's 14 additions (UNSENT)

Verified 2026-11-17 UTC against EPA Region 4's June 12, 2026 decision document, pp. 6–7 (PDF pp. 7–8): https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2026-10/final-fl-2024-303-d-list-decision-document.pdf

## Findings
- Two chlorophyll-a assessment combinations: ACME North and South Sectors. EPA says annual geometric means exceeded criteria too often and the state did not establish good cause for excluding them.
- Four dissolved-oxygen combinations: EPA objects to automatically discarding older monitoring data, especially where reduced sampling can cause delisting without evidence of improved water quality. EPA says these waters had not demonstrated a change sufficient to justify removal.
- Eight lead combinations: Florida excluded paired lead/hardness observations where hardness was below the lab practical quantitation limit and that limit exceeded 25 mg/L. The lead criterion varies with hardness. EPA independently used the laboratory practical quantitation limit as a hardness input and identified eight waters failing lead criteria. This is a methodological disagreement about assessment inputs, not proof of a new pollution release or a finding that all individual samples exceed a single fixed threshold.
- Appendix D's 27 pending items remain separate; this note does not reclassify them as additions.

## Evidence boundaries and questions for qualified review
EPA's hardness substitution is described but the sample-level sensitivity, actual paired observations, uncertainty and alternative criteria calculations were not independently reproduced. Ask a qualified water-quality reviewer to evaluate how substituting the practical quantitation limit affects exceedance determinations for each lead assessment unit, and whether later representative monitoring changes any dissolved-oxygen conclusions. Do not assert Florida acted unlawfully or that the listed waters have recently deteriorated.
