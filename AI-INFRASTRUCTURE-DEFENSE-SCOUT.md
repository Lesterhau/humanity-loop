# Defensive AI-swarm and critical-systems security Scout

**Status:** Active Humanity Loop *read-only scouting lens*, established October 23, 2026; no separate bot, scheduled job, security testing on live systems, or outside correspondence.  
**Purpose:** Find, independently verify, amplify and appropriately connect defensive work that helps **critical infrastructure, banks, public services and large-scale digital systems** withstand coordinated AI-enabled threats and unsafe agent deployments.

## Why this is a real public-interest priority

- CISA and allied agencies' May 2026 agentic-AI security guidance addresses autonomous agent privileges, identity, unexpected behavior, auditability and oversight in mission-critical environments.
- Australia's Cyber Security Centre's July 2026 guidance describes faster vulnerability exploitation and recommends least-privilege identity, isolation, secure baselines, logs, narrow preapproved reversible responses and controlled/human-supervised code review.
- The Bank for International Settlements' September 2026 analysis documents frontier-AI cyber risks for financial institutions, including compressed remediation windows and third-party concentration, alongside defensive opportunities.

**Careful distinction:** Coordinated malicious AI swarms are a serious risk scenario, but specific future attacks, attribution to AI, number of active swarm operators and exploit success cannot be inferred merely from these advisories. Do not assert a live breach or imminent specific event without independent evidence.

## Search for actionable defenses, not another list of threats

Qualify and compare projects working on:

1. **Agent tool and authorization boundaries:** least-privilege execution, identity, signed delegation, approval on consequential actions, prompt-injection containment and independently enforced tool policies
2. **Protective detection and response:** defensive anomaly detection, threat-intelligence sharing, operational monitoring, incident containment, tested graceful degradation and recovery
3. **Cross-provider and supply-chain resilience:** third-party risk, secure updates, trustworthy dependency reporting, isolated environments, backup and cyber-physical fail-safe operation
4. **Financial-system resilience:** anti-fraud defenses, payment-integrity checks, secure interbank/critical vendor coordination, resilience testing and response readiness
5. **Open, accessible defensive research:** freely usable evaluation benchmarks, open security guidance, volunteer programs, maintained defensive software and public-interest research funding

Prioritize concrete assets, verifiable maintainers, open contribution opportunities and underserved operators. Beware expensive products presented as solutions without independent evidence, projects seeking sensitive telemetry and any offer requiring unauthorized access to live infrastructure.

## Initial bounded Scout investigation

Within an existing due Scout cycle or a specifically authorized one-off read-only research slot:

1. Pick **one** of the candidates above. Verify its present project scope, contributor guidelines, maintainer activity, existing similar solutions and a small evidence-backed unmet public need.
2. Compare its published defensive approach to CISA/ACSC and the financial-sector guidance. Require source dates, geographic relevance, falsification criteria, and beneficiary value.
3. Ask 10th Man: *Could our assistance duplicate a well-resourced security organization, introduce risk, or mislead operators about tool effectiveness?*
4. If genuinely useful, choose one bounded contribution: public documentation accuracy, translating maintained guidance with permission, grant/community matchmaking, open educational material, or defensive code review on a repository whose maintainers explicitly permit contributions. Record ownership and handoff to a qualified specialist.
5. Record **no actionable gap** honestly if the landscape is already well covered. Do not solicit or contact critical-system operators simply because they may be vulnerable.
6. Measure accepted corrections, maintained open assets, real operator uptake and independently verifiable outcomes, not numbers of security alerts or speculative vulnerabilities.

## Safety boundary

**Defensive public-information research only unless separately and explicitly authorized.** Do not scan, probe, exploit, enumerate, benchmark or target live banks, power grids, telecom networks, hospitals, critical vendors or other organizations. Do not publish exploitable weaknesses, attack playbooks, system access details or weaponized automation. Security coordination and disclosure follow the responsible maintainer's policy and Humanity Loop's existing governance.

This is a **cross-cutting priority applied to existing geography × language × domain Scout Mesh work**, not a seventh cell, unbounded crawler or new paid agent. Maintain the six existing cells' overdue recovery obligations and record actual completed research rather than claim timestamps.
