# Civic Engagement Opportunity Workstream

Humanity Loop may support civic participation by reducing friction and surfacing legitimate opportunities for people to participate in public life **without advocating for a political outcome**.

## Mission

Increase meaningful access to civic participation by finding concrete opportunities, identifying avoidable barriers, and improving usability/accessibility.

Examples:
- public-comment periods;
- city/county/school/transit/planning meetings;
- participatory budgeting;
- boards and commissions with vacancies;
- election-worker needs;
- community surveys and listening sessions;
- jury/civic-service education gaps;
- mutual-aid and neighborhood projects;
- public hearings;
- government consultations;
- opportunities to submit testimony;
- community advisory panels;
- citizen-science/public-data projects;
- civic/nonprofit volunteer roles;
- legislative processes where public comment is formally accepted;
- local/special elections and other low-awareness civic processes.

## Neutrality rule

The system may:
- identify participation opportunities;
- explain eligibility, deadlines, procedures, and accessibility;
- reduce paperwork/friction;
- compare official participation channels;
- identify structural barriers.

It may **not**:
- recommend a candidate, party, ballot choice, or ideological outcome;
- rank political actors;
- target users based on inferred political preference;
- manipulate turnout for one side.

## Agent chain

### Civic Opportunity Scout
Finds time-bounded participation opportunities.

### Civic Friction Auditor
Asks why eligible people are not participating:
- poor discoverability;
- difficult language;
- inaccessible scheduling;
- transportation;
- disability/access barriers;
- confusing forms;
- unnecessary in-person requirements;
- language access;
- unclear eligibility.

### Civic Access Builder
Builds or proposes neutral interventions:
- plain-language explainers;
- deadline/calendar feeds;
- accessible forms;
- translation;
- participation checklists;
- meeting/accessibility summaries;
- routing to existing organizations when they already solve the problem.

## Structured opportunity record

Each opportunity should include:
- opportunity;
- why participation matters procedurally;
- who is eligible;
- effort/time required;
- deadline;
- participation methods;
- accessibility/language options;
- likely participation friction;
- official source;
- outcome/feedback path where available.

## Research opportunity

A future **Civic Opportunity Index** could measure the gap between formal participation rights and practical participation friction across jurisdictions or institutions.

Any such index must remain descriptive and neutral. It should measure access/friction, not score political outcomes or tell people how to vote.
