# Public-information integrity and influence-operations Scout

**Status:** Active, research-only priority beginning 2026-10-27. No live network investigation, political persuasion campaign, attribution allegation, polling, hacking, targeted person-level profiling, or automated social reply authorized.

## Purpose

Reduce public harm from covert foreign and domestic influence operations, inauthentic coordinated networks, religious or ethnic hate propaganda, election-related falsehoods, and manipulation of information distribution. Develop evidence-backed public tools, media-literacy materials, factual error corrections, responsible referrals and partnerships with credible nonpartisan researchers.

This is a **cross-cutting Scout Mesh lens**, not a new scheduled task or a declaration that any particular actor committed misconduct. Do not use Humanity Loop to advance the owner's personal political preferences: apply the same standards regardless of government, political camp, platform owner or targeted group.

## Named research priorities from owner

1. **Foreign-state influence and transparency, including Israeli and Russian activities relevant to U.S. information ecosystems.** Read U.S. DOJ Foreign Agents Registration Act filings, disclosed principals, actual contracts, materials and expenditures; compare with other authoritative primary findings. Distinguish **lawful, disclosed lobbying, diplomacy or issue advocacy** from covert direction, inauthentic coordination or proven violations. Never treat Jewish identity, Israeli nationality, or disagreement on Israel/Palestine as evidence of wrongdoing. The owner specifically wants claims of state-linked Islamophobic propaganda independently investigated rather than presumed.
2. **Russian and other documented foreign election-related information activity.** ODNI's public 2024 election-security statements provide a historical example of an assessed Russian influence threat. They are NOT evidence of a specific new 2026 campaign without timely corroboration. Research countermeasures and accessible threat-intelligence explanations rather than produce partisan vote-persuasion material.
3. **Domestic platform influence, AI accounts, and concentration of information power (including X/Elon Musk as a case study).** Independently research recommender-system transparency, access policies for researchers, platform moderation practices, sponsored amplification, bot detection, dataset gaps and conflicts of interest. Legal political speech, platform ownership, algorithmic amplification and unlawful foreign election interference are **different categories**; do not casually equate them or allege criminal wrongdoing without corroborated case-specific evidence.
4. **Coordinated inauthentic behavior, synthetic media, hate-speech amplification and propaganda networks across languages/platforms.** Investigate anti-Muslim and antisemitic hate with equally rigorous evidence, also other targeted groups. Do not blame broad religious or ethnic communities for governmental or network-operator conduct.

## Standards for a source-backed candidate

Every candidate must state the **specific testable claim, date, actor classification, platform, verified provenance, primary sources, corroboration, competing explanations, confidence level, what remains unknown, and a narrow public-benefit intervention**. Record the difference between first-party platform allegations, independently reproduced findings, official allegations, regulator decisions, court findings, and speculative claims. Never turn unsourced screenshots or viral allegations into verified attribution.

Original-source starting points:
- DOJ FARA public disclosure and obligations: https://www.justice.gov/nsd-fara
- DOJ FARA guidance including exemptions: https://www.justice.gov/nsd-fara/frequently-asked-questions
- ODNI public 2024 foreign-influence assessment (historical context, not 2026 evidence): https://www.odni.gov/files/FMIC/documents/ODNI-Election-Security-Update-20241021.pdf
- Platform transparency archives, academic replication material, election administration authorities and documented digital-forensics methodology; validate freshness, ownership and scope before quoting.

## Bounded first-cycle work

1. Choose **one** neutral empirical research question, e.g. "Which publicly disclosed foreign influence contracts include online-content activities, and what do the actual filings permit us to conclude?" or "Which independently validated detection methods identify coordinated inauthentic networks without silencing legitimate activism?"
2. Check independent existing public-interest initiatives and unmet need before proposing a new tool or a claim against an actor.
3. Generate **one safe output**: evidence explainer, source index, neutral transparency comparison, publication correction, public reporting guide, or an opt-in introduction to a qualified research group.
4. Ask a second reviewer to attack the inference, including false-positive risks and lawful expression impacts; treat conclusions as provisional when the evidence is ambiguous.
5. Prefer policy-neutral information integrity, researcher access and user agency over content designed to change how specific demographics vote. Do not target electoral audiences with persuasive posts or create a political bot network.

## Safety and reporting

No account infiltration, private-data scraping, live system testing, coordinated mass reporting, brigading, covert persona operations, automated mentions/DMs, or credential reuse. No publication of individual identities based on unverified bot classification. Responsible disclosure for security vulnerabilities; consult platform rules and verified account authority before any externally consequential step.

Only independently verified **public-benefit discoveries** may become MOTHER draft candidates, with balanced attribution, corrections channel, citations, explicit AI disclosure and human review. The output target is reduced vulnerability to manipulation and useful public trust, not an engagement metric or partisan victory.
