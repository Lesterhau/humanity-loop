# Planetary Systems Division

AI scaling increases the urgency of reducing energy, water, material, and ecosystem costs. Humanity Loop therefore treats planetary-system repair and low-impact computation as a major portfolio priority.

## Mission

Accelerate solutions that:
1. reduce energy consumed per unit of useful computation;
2. reduce water and material intensity of computing infrastructure;
3. expand genuinely low-carbon/zero-emission energy;
4. improve storage, transmission, grid flexibility, and demand response;
5. reduce methane and other high-impact emissions;
6. remove durable CO2 where removal is measurable and additional;
7. improve industrial decarbonization;
8. restore ocean/coastal ecosystems and coral resilience;
9. reduce toxic pollution and critical-mineral harms;
10. improve circularity, reuse, heat recovery, and waste reduction.

## Elastic replication

Planetary agents may auto-scale through the bounded Foundry rules in ARCHITECTURE.md when:
- task backlog is high;
- opportunities are independently verified;
- compute budget permits;
- additional agents create expected marginal value.

They may not recursively expand without caps or inherited governance.

## Prioritization

Prefer interventions with:
- measurable avoided emissions/resource use;
- high additionality;
- scalable learning curves;
- rapid deployment;
- low ecological rebound risk;
- transparent lifecycle accounting;
- co-benefits for health/resilience.

Avoid treating speculative carbon claims as equivalent to measured physical outcomes.

## AI self-footprint

Humanity Loop should periodically estimate its own compute/resource footprint and compare it with expected project benefit.

If agent expansion creates substantial environmental cost without proportional benefit, the Workforce Allocator should reduce/reassign compute.


## Humanity Loop's own footprint: abate first, compensate second

Humanity Loop should not claim climate benefit while ignoring the energy, water, hardware, and infrastructure consumed by its own agent fleet.

### Immediate operating rule

1. **Measure with ranges, not fake precision.** Track available model/runtime/token/compute metadata and publish uncertainty when provider-level energy/water/carbon data are unavailable.
2. **Reduce first.** Prefer smaller models, shorter contexts, caching, batching, task skipping, efficient scheduling, and non-AI code/rules when they achieve the same outcome.
3. **Use cleaner timing/location where actually controllable.** Shift flexible workloads toward lower-carbon electricity periods or regions only when the infrastructure exposes credible signals and moving the workload does not create larger harms.
4. **Track marginal value per unit of compute.** More agents are justified only when expected public benefit exceeds their incremental resource cost.
5. **Compensate residual emissions only after reduction.** Any carbon-removal/offset claim must distinguish avoidance from removal and disclose additionality, durability, leakage, verification, and uncertainty.
6. **Do not call an unverified credit 'offsetting our footprint.'** If Humanity Loop cannot establish a credible footprint baseline and credible compensation, say so.

### Planetary balance sheet

Maintain a recurring estimate of:
- model/API usage where observable;
- estimated electricity range;
- estimated cooling-water range;
- estimated operational carbon range;
- major non-compute infrastructure;
- avoided compute from skipped/rejected tasks;
- verified environmental benefit from Humanity Loop interventions;
- any purchased removals/credits if humans later authorize spending.

The goal is not performative carbon neutrality. The goal is to make the net environmental value of the system increasingly positive.

## Scouting beyond technology

Planetary Scouts must search for both technological and non-technological interventions, including:

- regulatory and market-design changes;
- conservation and ecosystem-restoration practices;
- indigenous/local ecological knowledge;
- land-use and urban-design changes;
- diet/material substitution;
- behavioral and organizational practices;
- financing/insurance innovations;
- permitting and interconnection reform;
- maintenance and efficiency practices;
- community-owned infrastructure;
- demand reduction and sufficiency strategies;
- governance arrangements that improve stewardship.

A solution does not get extra priority merely because it uses AI, advanced materials, or a shiny machine.
