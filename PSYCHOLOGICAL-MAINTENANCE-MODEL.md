# Psychological Maintenance as Public Infrastructure — v1

## Decision

Humanity Loop should **not** model universal psychological maintenance as recurring psychotherapy for every person.

The strongest current design is a **voluntary stepped public-infrastructure model** that makes low-cost mental-health maintenance universally available while concentrating scarce human clinical capacity where need is demonstrated.

The infrastructure goal is analogous to public health:
- universal availability and prevention;
- low-friction early support;
- targeted escalation;
- professional care when clinically appropriate;
- crisis/specialist care when required.

It is not a program for changing ideology, personality, religion, politics, or conformity.

## Why not universal therapy?

A 2025 systematic review/meta-analysis of 17 randomized trials (10,809 participants) found no statistically significant preventive benefit from universal CBT programs delivered to unscreened general populations for depression, anxiety, or quality of life. That is a strong reason **not** to build the system around mandatory or routine universal CBT.

Reference:
- Bermudez MB, Gosmann NP, Martins DS, et al. (2025). *Universal prevention programs for depression and anxiety disorders in children and adults: a systematic review and meta-analysis.* Trends in Psychiatry and Psychotherapy. https://consensus.app/papers/universal-prevention-programs-for-depression-and-anxiety-bermudez-gosmann/a9ef7b37a1195916a13c9f3249b4f9b1/

Targeted prevention appears more promising than blanket universal prevention in multiple reviews, and scalable digital resilience interventions can produce small favorable effects in nonclinical adults.

Reference:
- Schäfer S, Schaubruch LM, Kunzler AM, et al. (2024). *Digital interventions to promote psychological resilience: a systematic review and meta-analysis.* NPJ Digital Medicine 7. 101 studies / 20,010 participants; pooled effects favored digital interventions for distress (SMD -0.24), positive mental health (0.27), and resilience factors (0.31). https://consensus.app/papers/digital-interventions-to-promote-psychological-schäfer-schaubruch/dc6da9815b605d5d9d702df1114a5955/

## Global constraint

The binding problem is not merely intervention design. It is capacity.

WHO's Mental Health Atlas 2024 reports:
- global median specialized mental-health workforce: 13.5 per 100,000;
- only 1.1–2.4 per 100,000 in low- and lower-middle-income settings versus 67.2 in high-income settings;
- mental health receives about 2.1% of government health expenditure globally;
- median mental-health spending ranges from roughly US$0.04/person in low-income countries to US$65.89/person in high-income countries;
- fewer than 10% of responding countries have fully transitioned to community-based care.

WHO's *World mental health today* estimates only about 9% of people with depression receive minimally adequate treatment globally.

Official sources:
- WHO Mental Health Atlas 2024: https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240114487
- WHO World mental health today: https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240113817
- WHO Special Initiative for Mental Health: https://www.who.int/initiatives/who-special-initiative-for-mental-health

The WHO Special Initiative is useful as a systems-expansion benchmark, not a treatment-cost estimate: by end-2025 it reported >90 million additional people with access to newly available local services and roughly 2.6 million additional people gaining access per US$1 million invested. "Access" must not be misrepresented as completed treatment or clinical benefit.

## Evidence-supported service layers

### Tier 0 — universal availability, not universal treatment

Available to everyone, voluntary, self-directed unless the person asks for more.

Components may include:
- evidence-based mental-health literacy;
- brief resilience/emotional-regulation skills;
- behavioral activation / healthy-routine supports;
- stress-management tools;
- social-connection/community-navigation resources;
- sleep/activity/substance-risk education;
- self-assessment and clear pathways to higher tiers;
- culturally adapted digital and offline materials.

No diagnosis is required.

The system should avoid presenting generic universal CBT as proven population-wide prevention.

**Human intensity:** near zero per user after development, except moderation/support/accessibility.

### Tier 1 — selective / indicated early support

For people who opt in because of:
- elevated distress;
- recognized risk factors;
- functional decline;
- recent adverse event;
- self-request;
- validated screening signal when screening is appropriate.

Possible supports:
- guided digital modules;
- brief behavioral activation/problem-solving;
- structured peer/community navigation;
- short check-ins by trained non-specialists;
- navigation to social services where material conditions are the actual bottleneck.

**Human intensity:** low.

### Tier 2 — task-shared structured brief care

Use trained and supervised nonspecialist workers for appropriate common mental-health problems.

Evidence:
- Karyotaki E, Araya R, Kessler RC, et al. (2022), JAMA Psychiatry: individual-patient-data meta-analysis of 11 trials / 4,145 participants in LMICs found task-shared psychological interventions associated with lower depressive symptoms (Hedges g 0.32), higher response (OR 2.11), and higher remission (OR 1.87) versus controls. https://consensus.app/papers/association-of-taskshared-psychological-interventions-karyotaki-araya/bfd3216db8ec57ecb6d8411745a81973/

Task-sharing requires:
- defined scope;
- competency assessment;
- supervision;
- escalation protocols;
- worker support/burnout protection;
- cultural/language adaptation.

It is not "unlicensed therapy with no oversight."

### Tier 3 — integrated primary-care / collaborative care

For moderate or persistent needs, comorbidity, medication management, or cases needing coordinated clinical oversight.

Evidence:
- Schillok H, Gensichen J, Panagioti M, et al. (2025), JAMA Psychiatry: IPD meta-analysis of 35 datasets / 20,046 participants found collaborative-care components were associated with improved depression outcomes, with manual-based psychotherapy/family involvement among important treatment-strategy components. https://consensus.app/papers/effective-components-of-collaborative-care-for-schillok-gensichen/09f8822516955662a4fe598b85fb454a/

Core functions:
- primary-care identification;
- care manager;
- measurement-based follow-up;
- specialist consultation;
- medication / psychotherapy pathways;
- referral/escalation.

### Tier 4 — specialist / urgent / crisis care

For:
- severe/complex disorders;
- psychosis/mania;
- significant suicide/self-harm risk;
- severe substance-use complexity;
- diagnostic uncertainty or treatment resistance;
- safeguarding concerns;
- need for specialist medication or intensive therapy.

Human clinical authority remains human.

## Peer/community support: optional, not foundational clinical treatment

Peer support can improve some recovery outcomes, but evidence is heterogeneous and clinical-symptom effects are limited.

Reference:
- Lyons N, Cooper C, Lloyd-Evans B. (2021). *A systematic review and meta-analysis of group peer support interventions for people experiencing mental health conditions.* BMC Psychiatry 21. Eight trials / 2,131 participants; small improvement in overall recovery, limited evidence for clinical symptoms, and insufficient evidence for routine universal implementation. https://consensus.app/papers/a-systematic-review-and-metaanalysis-of-group-peer-support-lyons-cooper/436f70fe557b5408b0f86be90dbbeba9/

Use peer support for:
- belonging;
- navigation;
- recovery support;
- lived-experience mentorship;
- community reintegration.

Do not use it as a cheap substitute for indicated clinical care.

## Delivery comparison

| Delivery model | Best role | Strength | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Licensed specialist expansion | Tiers 3–4 | depth, diagnosis, complexity | workforce/cost bottleneck |
| Task-sharing | Tier 2 | greatly expands brief-care capacity | needs training/supervision/scope control |
| Primary-care collaborative care | Tier 3 | embeds mental health into existing care | requires primary-care capacity + specialist consultation |
| Peer/community support | Tiers 0–2 adjunct | belonging, navigation, recovery | inconsistent clinical-symptom evidence |
| Guided digital | Tiers 0–2 | scalable, low marginal cost | engagement, access, privacy, variable durability |
| Self-directed digital | Tier 0 / some Tier 1 | highest scale, lowest human burden | modest average effects; not sufficient for complex cases |

The architecture should combine these rather than choose one.

## Capacity model

The reference calculator is `scripts/psychological_maintenance_capacity.py`.

It models a population through four demand-bearing service tiers and estimates:
- participants by tier;
- annual service cost under low/base/high assumptions;
- nonspecialist FTE need;
- specialist/care-manager FTE need;
- sessions/contacts;
- workload sensitivity.

Default inputs are explicitly **scenario assumptions**, not claims about universal treatment costs.

Use the calculator for:
- national planning;
- city/health-system planning;
- global order-of-magnitude scenarios;
- sensitivity analysis.

Do not publish a single global "price tag" without showing participation, unit-cost, wage/productivity, and service-intensity assumptions.


### Illustrative world-scale sensitivity

To make the scale legible, the reference calculator can be run against a modeled population of **8.2 billion**, the UN-reported global population level for 2024. This is an order-of-magnitude demonstration, not a claim that all 8.2 billion people are eligible, participate, or face identical costs.

UN population source:
- United Nations DESA, *World Population Prospects 2024*: https://www.un.org/development/desa/pd/world-population-prospects-2024

Using the calculator's deliberately broad low/base/high assumptions:

| Scenario | Annual system cost | Cost / modeled population | Nonspecialist FTE | Specialist / care-manager FTE |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Low | ~$7.38B | ~$0.90 | ~157,000 | ~30,750 |
| Base | ~$43.87B | ~$5.35 | ~478,000 | ~133,250 |
| High | ~$186.96B | ~$22.80 | ~1,640,000 | ~481,750 |

These figures are **sensitivity outputs, not empirical global cost estimates**.

They assume mutually exclusive annual highest-service tiers and 1,200 productive service hours per FTE. The main purpose is to expose the consequence of assumptions:

- Tier-0 marginal cost matters enormously because it touches the largest population share.
- Small changes in Tier-2/3 participation substantially alter workforce demand.
- Specialist capacity remains a bottleneck even in a task-sharing architecture.
- Digital/community infrastructure can expand reach, but cannot erase the need for supervised human care.
- Country implementation must substitute local wage, utilization, age structure, burden, digital-access, and service-intensity data before any budget claim.

This scale sensitivity should be compared with WHO's reported global treatment gap and workforce disparity, not mistaken for a proposal to buy a standardized service package for every person.

## Rights / failure modes

### Privacy
- default to minimal collection;
- separate identity from screening where possible;
- no commercial advertising profile;
- no sale of mental-health data;
- short retention for raw self-assessment data unless clinically needed;
- user-visible audit/access/delete controls;
- strong protections against employer/insurer/law-enforcement repurposing.

### Coercion
Prohibit:
- compulsory participation as a condition of employment, education, benefits, immigration, insurance, or policing;
- hidden screening;
- punitive scoring;
- automated denial of opportunity based on mental-health inferences;
- ideological conformity goals.

### Stigma
Use normalizing "maintenance / skills / support" access without implying everyone is ill.

### Digital harms
Monitor:
- symptom worsening;
- inappropriate reassurance;
- dependency;
- privacy breaches;
- crisis misses;
- hallucinated clinical guidance;
- exclusion from language/disability/digital-access barriers.

### Workforce harms
Monitor:
- task-shared worker burnout;
- role drift;
- inadequate supervision;
- wage suppression;
- replacing scarce clinicians where clinicians are actually required.

### Social-determinants failure
Do not psychologize structural harm.

If housing, violence, debt, discrimination, food insecurity, work conditions, or another material constraint is the dominant problem, the maintenance system should route to material/social support rather than imply emotional skills are the solution.

## No ideology promise

This system must not claim that therapy:
- makes people politically moderate;
- removes prejudice;
- changes religion;
- changes ideology;
- makes people morally "better."

Those are distinct research questions and often normative ones.

Population outcomes here are mental-health / functioning / access / resilience outcomes.

## Pilot v1 — Voluntary Stepped Maintenance Pilot

**Status:** protocol-ready concept; do not run without appropriate ethics/clinical/governance review.

### Population

Adults in one willing institution/community or primary-care network.

Target enrollment for feasibility:
- 1,200 participants;
- broad adult inclusion;
- exclude people requiring immediate emergency intervention from the low-intensity intervention pathway and route them appropriately.

### Design

Randomized pragmatic pilot:

**Arm A — Stepped Maintenance**
- Tier-0 digital/offline maintenance library;
- optional brief periodic self-check;
- participant-triggered help request;
- Tier-1 guided support if indicated/requested;
- Tier-2 task-shared brief intervention where appropriate;
- Tier-3 collaborative-care referral/escalation.

**Arm B — Information / navigation control**
- high-quality mental-health information;
- ordinary existing care pathways;
- crisis and emergency information;
- no proactive stepped-maintenance layer.

No participant is denied ordinary care.

### Primary outcomes

At baseline, 8 weeks, 3 months, 6 months:
- psychological distress / symptom burden using validated short measures appropriate to the population;
- positive mental health / wellbeing;
- functional impairment;
- unmet need / time-to-appropriate-support.

### Secondary outcomes

- uptake by tier;
- engagement and dropout;
- referral completion;
- wait time;
- participant-reported usefulness;
- adverse effects / symptom worsening;
- crisis escalations and missed escalations;
- privacy incidents;
- task-shared worker workload/burnout;
- cost per participant offered Tier 0;
- cost per participant receiving Tier 1/2/3;
- equity by language, disability, socioeconomic status, age, sex/gender, and other locally appropriate dimensions.

### Predefined safety escalation

Immediate clinical/human escalation for:
- acute suicide/self-harm risk;
- psychosis/mania signal;
- abuse/safeguarding issue;
- severe functional deterioration;
- participant request for clinician review.

No LLM independently decides emergency disposition.

### Stop / redesign conditions

Pause or redesign the intervention if any of the following occurs:
1. serious privacy/security breach;
2. evidence of coercive use by the host institution;
3. materially higher symptom worsening or adverse events than control;
4. crisis escalation failures above the predefined safety threshold;
5. subgroup evidence of material inequitable harm/access;
6. task-shared worker supervision cannot be maintained;
7. participant complaints show the system is substituting low-intensity support for needed clinical care;
8. engagement is too low to plausibly generate the intended benefit;
9. independent monitoring determines the intervention lacks equipoise or ethical acceptability.

### Success threshold for scale decision

Do **not** require every outcome to improve.

Advance to a larger replication only if:
- primary safety thresholds are met;
- at least one preregistered primary outcome shows a meaningful improvement or clear access benefit;
- no credible evidence of net harm;
- service-capacity/cost data support feasibility;
- subgroup results do not show material unmitigated inequity;
- results survive independent statistical/clinical review.

## What Humanity Loop should build next

1. evidence table with GRADE/risk-of-bias fields;
2. localized capacity/cost scenarios;
3. ethics/privacy threat model;
4. supervision and competency protocol for task-sharing;
5. preregistration-ready pilot protocol;
6. external clinical/public-health review before any human-subject implementation.

Humanity Loop should research and design this infrastructure. It should **not** autonomously enroll or treat people.
