# Unknown Unknowns & Question Discovery

Humanity Loop must not assume the most important opportunities are already represented in mainstream research agendas.

A core Scout function is therefore **question discovery**: finding important possibilities that institutions are not yet asking about.

## Rumsfeld matrix

For each domain, explicitly map:

1. **Known knowns** — evidence and mechanisms we understand reasonably well.
2. **Known unknowns** — questions we know remain unresolved.
3. **Unknown knowns** — useful knowledge that exists but is siloed, local, tacit, ignored, untranslated, proprietary, indigenous, or outside the dominant discipline.
4. **Unknown unknowns** — possibilities absent from the current question set.

The fourth category cannot be searched directly. Humanity Loop looks for its fingerprints.

## Methods

### Assumption inversion
List the field's assumptions and reverse them.

Examples:
- What if scarcity is actually a waste-stream problem?
- What if the solution is biological rather than mechanical?
- What if the "waste" material is the feedstock?
- What if distributed repair beats centralized replacement?

### Negative-space search
Ask what data, geography, population, material, failure mode, or stakeholder is consistently missing.

### Anomaly hunting
Prioritize outcomes that appear too good/bad/cheap/fast to fit the prevailing model, then verify aggressively.

### Cross-domain analogy
Search for solved problems with the same structural shape in unrelated fields.

### Failure mining
Review failed grants, abandoned prototypes, retracted hypotheses, old patents, discontinued products, and projects that failed for timing/cost reasons that may have changed.

### Weird-but-testable channel
Reserve scouting capacity for unconventional but falsifiable ideas:
- waste-derived fuels/materials;
- unusual reef substrates or ecological restoration methods;
- low-tech repair techniques;
- neglected biological processes;
- repurposed industrial byproducts;
- local inventions that look crude but solve real constraints.

"Weird" earns investigation, not belief.

### Question storms
Before starting a project, generate questions that would change the strategy if answered differently. Rank by expected information value.

### Outside-mainstream sampling
Deliberately sample:
- local-language media;
- small universities;
- makers/inventors;
- indigenous/local ecological knowledge;
- trade journals;
- field practitioners;
- repair communities;
- grant finalists;
- patents;
- student work;
- neglected regions.

## Unknown-unknown escalation

A surprising signal should not be amplified merely because it is novel.

Escalation requires:
1. provenance;
2. independent verification;
3. mechanism plausibility or demonstrable result;
4. comparison with existing alternatives;
5. tests designed to falsify it;
6. 10th-Man review for large resource mobilization.

## Portfolio rule

Reserve a meaningful minority of scout capacity for question discovery and anomalous signals instead of optimizing every cycle around known mainstream research questions.
