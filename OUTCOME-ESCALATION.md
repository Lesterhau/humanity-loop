# Outcome Escalation Protocol

Humanity Loop does not allow useful work to disappear into an indefinite "monitoring" state.

## Core rule

Every externally dependent action must have:
- an owner/target;
- expected response window;
- next review date;
- stale threshold;
- escalation path;
- closure condition.

The threshold is domain-sensitive. A safety-critical problem may require hours; a routine standards or administrative issue may reasonably require weeks.

## Default urgency classes

### P0 — imminent safety / severe time-critical harm
- first recheck: hours
- escalation: immediate alternate channel / responsible safety authority
- public transparency only after verification and when disclosure does not increase harm

### P1 — high-consequence public-service or safety defect
- first recheck: 1–3 days
- factual follow-up if unresolved
- alternate responsible channel if no meaningful response

### P2 — material public guidance / institutional issue
- first recheck: ~7 days
- one reasonable follow-up
- alternate oversight/maintainer channel if still unresolved

### P3 — routine improvement / low-urgency contribution
- first recheck: 14–30 days
- close, defer, or escalate based on expected value

These are defaults, not rigid SLAs.

## Escalation ladder

1. **Report / submit**
2. **Verify receipt**
3. **Recheck outcome**
4. **Reasonable follow-up**
5. **Escalate internally to a higher responsible level, with the prior correspondence and receipts**
6. **Allow that higher level a reasonable opportunity to resolve it**
7. **Alternate oversight/responsible channel when warranted**
8. **Public transparency packet**
9. **MOTHER visibility escalation**
10. **Close / retire / supersede / blocked-with-reason**

### Alternate responsible channel

Examples:
- maintainer → project lead/foundation;
- agency program office → ombudsman/inspector general;
- public guidance owner → appropriate oversight office;
- standards defect → standards body;
- research-integrity issue → editor/publisher/institutional integrity channel.

Never choose channels based on partisan alignment. Choose them based on institutional responsibility.

## MOTHER escalation

MOTHER may publicize a repeatedly stalled issue when:
- the underlying claim is independently verified;
- the original responsible party had a reasonable opportunity to respond;
- public visibility has plausible benefit;
- publication does not create a material safety/privacy risk;
- the post is factual and includes receipts;
- political/government items remain neutral and do not tell people which party, candidate, or policy outcome to support.

A MOTHER escalation should state:
- what was found;
- when it was reported;
- what evidence was supplied;
- what response, if any, occurred;
- what remains unresolved;
- where the public record can be inspected.

## Intentional resistance vs ordinary delay

Do not infer bad faith merely from silence.

Repeated delay becomes stronger evidence of resistance only when there is:
- acknowledged responsibility;
- clear ability to act;
- multiple reasonable opportunities;
- contradictory explanations;
- documented refusal despite verified harm;
- or a repeated pattern across similar cases.

Label incompetence, resistance, neglect, and ordinary queue delay separately when evidence allows. Otherwise use "unresolved."

## Closure

An Outcome Tracker must choose one:
- resolved;
- partially resolved;
- rejected with reason;
- superseded;
- no longer useful;
- intentionally closed;
- blocked;
- public-escalation active.

There is no permanent "still monitoring" status.


## Assume chaos before malice

Humanity Loop should not create a culture where public servants, maintainers, or institutional staff fear being publicly targeted for ordinary mistakes.

Before public escalation:
1. assume mistake, overload, broken process, unclear ownership, or team chaos are plausible;
2. provide a reasonable factual follow-up;
3. if unresolved, escalate **inside the responsible organization** to a higher level with receipts and the history of prior attempts;
4. give that level a reasonable opportunity to act;
5. only then consider external oversight or MOTHER public visibility.

Skip steps only when delay itself creates significant safety/harm risk.

Public escalation should target the **unresolved problem and institutional process**, not humiliation of an individual employee.

Evidence of intentional obstruction must be established rather than inferred from slow response.
