# Inner Development Pilot — Evidence-Backed v1

## Purpose

Design a low-cost, culturally portable, worldview-pluralistic pilot that tests whether a small bundle of trainable wellbeing practices can improve:

- emotional regulation / distress;
- positive wellbeing;
- connectedness;
- compassion / self-compassion;
- meaning / purpose;
- prosocial action.

Planetary concern may be measured as an **exploratory** outcome, but the intervention must not be designed to produce a prescribed political, religious, or ideological position.

This is an intervention-research protocol, not a claim that "inner development" is one validated construct.

## Existing frameworks / non-duplication map

### Center for Healthy Minds / Healthy Minds Framework

The Center for Healthy Minds organizes trainable wellbeing around:
- awareness;
- connection;
- insight;
- purpose.

This is highly adjacent to Humanity Loop's intended domain and argues against inventing a new conceptual vocabulary merely for branding.

Official source:
https://www.centerhealthyminds.org/about/

### Inner Development Goals

The Inner Development Goals (IDG) initiative currently organizes its open guide around five dimensions:
- Being;
- Thinking;
- Relating;
- Collaborating;
- Acting.

Its current Guide describes the framework as a globally co-created, evolving map rather than a definitive truth and reports input from roughly 20,000 voices across cultures/disciplines.

Official source:
https://innerdevelopmentgoals.org/guide/

Humanity Loop should treat IDG as a **framework/community-of-practice reference**, not as proof that any specific intervention changes health or societal outcomes.

### Wellbeing-intervention evidence

A 2026 network meta-analysis of 183 randomized trials (22,811 adults without diagnosed conditions) found that many wellbeing-focused interventions outperformed inactive controls. Combined exercise + psychological interventions showed the largest pooled effect (SMD 0.73, 95% CI 0.27–1.20); mindfulness, compassion, positive-psychology, yoga, and exercise interventions showed moderate pooled effects around 0.41–0.49. Nature-based interventions were not significantly superior in the network estimate, with limited/heterogeneous evidence. Risk of bias was often moderate-high and publication bias remained possible.

Reference:
- Wilkie L, Fisher Z, Geidel A, et al. (2026). *A systematic review and network meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials of well-being-focused interventions.* Nature Human Behaviour 10:715–726. https://consensus.app/papers/a-systematic-review-and-network-metaanalysis-of-wilkie-fisher/91ad4631348c505aae50d678de9a436a/

## Component evidence map

| Candidate | Evidence signal | Main limitation | Pilot status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brief awareness / mindfulness | Meta-analysis of 136 RCTs / 11,605 nonclinical adults found benefits vs no intervention, but weaker/no superiority vs specific active controls; effects heterogeneous | context-sensitive; not uniquely superior; adverse events possible | **Core, but optional/substitutable** |
| Compassion / self-compassion | Meta-analysis of 27 RCTs found improvements across several psychosocial outcomes; publication bias possible | heterogeneous intervention types/populations | **Core** |
| Awe / nature | Nature-exposure meta-analysis found improved positive and negative affect, but high heterogeneity/risk of bias; small newer awe RCTs are promising | evidence does not support universal strong causal claims | **Core choice option** |
| Meaning / purpose | Wellbeing meta-analytic literature supports positive-psychology/ACT-type approaches; Healthy Minds also treats purpose as trainable | direct population-level meaning-intervention evidence is less uniform | **Core, low-intensity** |
| Prosocial action / service | Large meta-analysis finds a modest prosociality–wellbeing association (r≈0.13) with substantial moderation | much evidence is correlational/heterogeneous; effects modest | **Core, small dose** |
| Movement / exercise | Stronger comparative signal in 2026 network meta-analysis, especially combined exercise + psychological approaches | accessibility and health constraints require adaptation | **Optional amplifier / substitute** |
| Secular/pluralistic ritual | plausible social/meaning mechanism | weaker direct intervention evidence; culturally sensitive | **Experimental only, not required** |

### Mindfulness evidence

Galante J, Friedrich C, Dawson AF, et al. (2021). *Mindfulness-based programmes for mental health promotion in adults in nonclinical settings: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomised controlled trials.* PLOS Medicine 18. 136 trials / 11,605 participants. Effects favored mindfulness programs over no intervention for several outcomes, but findings were heterogeneous; superiority over specific active controls was not established, and higher-risk populations tended to show larger effects.

https://consensus.app/papers/mindfulnessbased-programmes-for-mental-health-promotion-galante-friedrich/c141f0c70df85584b7e51269bec311b4/

### Meditation adverse effects

Farias M, Wallenkampf KC, Lucchetti G. (2020). *Adverse events in meditation practices and meditation-based therapies: a systematic review.* Acta Psychiatrica Scandinavica 142. Across 83 studies / 6,703 participants, reported adverse-event prevalence was 8.3% overall with large design-dependent variation; anxiety, depression, and cognitive anomalies were among commonly reported categories.

https://consensus.app/papers/adverse-events-in-meditation-practices-and-farias-wallenkampf/53d6fea31467545f97c5717a1f757b5a/

This is why the pilot must:
- disclose that contemplative practice can be uncomfortable/harmful for some people;
- permit immediate substitution;
- avoid intensive/long-duration meditation;
- provide clear stop/help pathways.

### Nature / awe evidence

Yao WF, Chen F, Wang S, Zhang X. (2021). *Impact of Exposure to Natural and Built Environments on Positive and Negative Affect: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.* Frontiers in Public Health 9. Twenty studies; natural exposure was associated with higher positive affect (SMD 0.61) and lower negative affect (SMD -0.47), but heterogeneity and risk of bias were high.

https://consensus.app/papers/impact-of-exposure-to-natural-and-built-environments-on-yao-chen/36e2762c65de5805b5c23c350c88a011/

Monroy M, Amster M, Eagle J, et al. (2025). *Awe reduces depressive symptoms and improves well-being in a randomized-controlled clinical trial.* Scientific Reports 15. A small long-COVID trial found promising wellbeing/stress/depression effects, but its sample and clinical context limit generalization.

https://consensus.app/papers/awe-reduces-depressive-symptoms-and-improves-wellbeing-in-monroy-amster/0bc5550f19e35f55986ffc49296f4d93/

### Compassion evidence

Ferrari M, Hunt C, Harrysunker A, et al. (2019). *Self-Compassion Interventions and Psychosocial Outcomes: a Meta-Analysis of RCTs.* Mindfulness. Twenty-seven RCTs showed improvements across multiple psychosocial outcomes, with heterogeneity and possible publication bias.

https://consensus.app/papers/selfcompassion-interventions-and-psychosocial-outcomes-ferrari-hunt/ee7c9b0efe0e5e44869f441771046f5d/

### Prosociality evidence

Hui B, Ng JCK, Berzaghi E, Cunningham-Amos LA, Kogan A. (2020). *Rewards of kindness? A meta-analysis of the link between prosociality and well-being.* Psychological Bulletin. Across 201 studies / 198,213 participants, the overall prosociality–wellbeing relationship was modest (r=0.13) and varied by type of wellbeing, prosociality, and population.

https://consensus.app/papers/rewards-of-kindness-a-metaanalysis-of-the-link-between-hui-ng/4d0148a1b6525eeda18a4e07927ac612/

## Pilot bundle

Working name: **Flourishing Micro-Bundle**

Duration: 6 weeks.

Target burden:
- 10–15 minutes/day average;
- one longer 20–30 minute practice/week;
- one small prosocial action/week.

### Daily / near-daily menu

Participants choose among functionally similar options.

#### 1. Awareness / grounding — 2–5 minutes

Examples:
- sensory grounding;
- mindful attention to sound/body;
- paced breathing;
- brief open awareness;
- non-meditative observation exercise.

**No requirement to close eyes, sit still, or meditate.**

#### 2. Compassion / self-compassion — 2–5 minutes

Examples:
- compassionate self-talk;
- perspective-taking;
- kindness reflection;
- supportive-letter microexercise.

No requirement for religious/metaphysical language.

#### 3. Insight / meaning — 2–5 minutes

Examples:
- values reflection;
- "what mattered today?";
- identify one action aligned with a personally chosen value;
- narrative meaning reflection.

The participant defines their own values.

### Twice weekly

#### 4. Awe / nature / vastness

Choose one:
- outdoor nature exposure;
- sky/weather/landscape attention;
- art/music/architecture;
- science/cosmos imagery;
- culturally meaningful place/object/story.

The mechanism being tested is attention to perceived vastness/novelty/connection, not supernatural belief.

### Weekly

#### 5. Prosocial action

One self-chosen, low-burden act that benefits another person/community without coercion.

Examples:
- help someone;
- volunteer briefly;
- express appreciation;
- share a useful resource;
- repair a relationship where appropriate;
- contribute to a community task.

Do not prescribe sacrifice or self-neglect.

### Optional amplifier

Accessible movement or exercise, ideally paired with nature when feasible.

Adapt for disability, health, environment, climate, safety, and cultural context.

### Experimental—not core

Pluralistic/shared ritual:
- group reflection;
- gratitude/commemoration;
- music;
- shared silence;
- culturally local noncoercive practice.

This component should only be tested after co-design. It must have a nonreligious equivalent and cannot require affirmation of metaphysical claims.

## Cultural portability rules

Translate **functions**, not scripts.

Each site should preserve target functions:
- attention;
- compassion;
- meaning;
- connection/awe;
- prosocial action;

while co-designing local forms.

Requirements:
- local-language delivery;
- local participant/community review;
- religious and secular options where relevant;
- disability/accessibility adaptation;
- no imported spiritual vocabulary required;
- no assumption that individualistic self-focus is culturally neutral;
- no claim that one tradition owns the practices.

## Pilot design

**Status:** protocol-ready feasibility/efficacy pilot. Human-subject execution requires appropriate ethics review and qualified research/clinical oversight.

### Sample

Provisional target: **300 adults** across at least 3 cultural/language contexts.

This is a planning target, not a final power calculation. Final N must be determined from:
- preregistered primary outcome;
- smallest effect of interest;
- expected attrition;
- clustering/site effects if applicable;
- statistician review.

### Arms

**Arm A — Flourishing Micro-Bundle**
- full modular 6-week bundle;
- participant choice within components;
- safety/substitution controls.

**Arm B — active information control**
- matched-duration evidence-based wellbeing education;
- no structured daily practice prescription;
- ordinary support/referral access remains available.

Do not use a no-care control where care is needed.

### Primary outcomes

Baseline, week 6, and week 12:
- validated positive-wellbeing measure (e.g. WHO-5 or equivalent);
- perceived stress/distress;
- social connectedness.

Choose exact scales before preregistration and check cultural validation.

### Secondary / mechanism outcomes

- emotional regulation;
- self-compassion;
- meaning/purpose;
- loneliness;
- positive/negative affect;
- nature connectedness / awe frequency;
- self-reported and, where feasible, behavioral prosociality;
- adherence and participant burden.

### Exploratory societal outcomes

May measure:
- generalized trust;
- willingness to cooperate across difference;
- stewardship / environmental concern.

These are exploratory only. Do **not** define political/ideological movement as success.

## Safety outcomes

Track weekly:
- new/worsening anxiety;
- depressive symptoms;
- dissociation/derealization;
- panic;
- sleep disruption;
- intrusive memories;
- mania/psychosis symptoms;
- suicidal ideation/self-harm;
- perceived coercion;
- spiritual/religious conflict;
- burden/irritation;
- adverse social consequences.

All serious clinical signals require human/qualified escalation according to the approved study protocol.

## Provisional stop / redesign conditions

These thresholds must be finalized with the ethics/safety team before enrollment.

Pause the intervention or affected component if:
1. any serious adverse event is plausibly intervention-related pending independent review;
2. moderate-or-worse adverse experiences are >5 percentage points more frequent than active control and the excess persists after verification;
3. a specific component shows a repeated pattern of symptom worsening;
4. the host institution uses participation or outcome data coercively;
5. privacy/security breach exposes sensitive participant data;
6. a cultural/site review finds the intervention is materially violating local consent/worldview norms;
7. dropout exceeds 40% overall or shows a major site/subgroup disparity suggesting burden or exclusion;
8. the intervention displaces indicated mental-health care;
9. an independent monitor determines continuation is no longer ethically justified.

## Success criterion

Proceed to a larger replication only if:
- no unresolved serious safety signal;
- retention/adherence demonstrates feasible burden;
- the preregistered primary outcome shows a meaningful favorable signal with uncertainty compatible with scale-up research;
- no material subgroup harm remains unmitigated;
- component acceptability is adequate across sites;
- the intervention can be delivered without worldview coercion;
- independent analysis/review agrees the result warrants replication.

A null result is useful. It should stop scale-up rather than trigger narrative reinterpretation.

## Dismantling / learning strategy

The first pilot tests the bundle.

If the bundle is promising, the next study should use factorial/dismantling methods to learn which components add value:
- awareness;
- compassion;
- meaning;
- awe/nature;
- prosocial action;
- movement.

Do not assume all components are necessary because the package worked.

## Relationship to IDG / Healthy Minds

Humanity Loop should not duplicate either framework.

Use:
- Healthy Minds' awareness/connection/insight/purpose as a strong adjacent scientific organizing reference;
- IDG's relational/systemic/cross-cultural framework as an implementation/community reference;
- Humanity Loop's contribution as **independent comparative testing, safety/falsification, cultural portability, and outcome transparency**.

## What this pilot does not claim

It does not claim:
- spiritual awakening;
- enlightenment;
- political moderation;
- ideological convergence;
- prejudice elimination;
- climate behavior change;
- population mental-health treatment;
- causal societal transformation.

Those would require separate evidence.

The question is narrower:

> Can a short, modular, culturally adapted, non-dogmatic set of practices safely and measurably improve wellbeing, regulation, connection, meaning, and prosocial capacity enough to justify a larger replication?
