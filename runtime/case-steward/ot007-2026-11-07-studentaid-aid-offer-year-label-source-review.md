## Direct observations

1. The live Federal Student Aid article **“How To Evaluate Your Aid Offers”** contains a table headed “Maximum Scheduled Award Amounts (subject to change).” Its Pell Grant row remains labeled **“Federal Pell Grant (2024–25) | up to $7,395”**, despite the current 2026–27 award year. The same page includes an explicit general warning that the Department is updating StudentAid.gov for program changes. The article was retrieved via the public web reader on 2026-11-07; the reader marked the page **crawled today**. Source: https://studentaid.gov/articles/evaluating-financial-aid-offers/ (table under “2. Add Up Your Federal Student Aid”).
2. The Department's **2026–27 Pell Grant maximum/minimum award letter**, GEN-26-01 (published 2026-01-30; updated 2026-02-18), specifies **$7,395 maximum** and **$740 minimum** for July 1, 2026–June 30, 2027. Therefore the article's *2024–25 year label* is stale, but its displayed **$7,395 maximum is numerically correct for 2026–27**. Source: https://fsapartners.ed.gov/knowledge-center/library/dear-colleague-letters/2026-01-30/2026-27-federal-pell-grant-maximum-and-minimum-award-amounts
3. The article also lists a **$3,772 TEACH Grant** maximum. Department FY27 sequestration guidance confirms **$3,772** for TEACH awards with first disbursement before 2027-10-01, subject to stated conditions. This check does **not** support treating that figure as a currently incorrect dollar amount. Source: https://fsapartners.ed.gov/fsa-print/publication/1007164
4. A separate Federal Student Aid public-facing Pell Grant article explicitly states **“For the 2026–27 award year, the maximum Pell Grant award is $7,395.”** Source: https://studentaid.gov/articles/dont-miss-out-on-pell-grants/
