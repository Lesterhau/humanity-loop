## Independently checked primary-source evidence

1. **Original Texas WIC bilingual handout:** https://texaswic.cart.com/Shared/PDF-Downloads/13-06-12103.pdf . A public PDF retrieval on November 8 exposed the one-page handout, stock 13-06-12103, revision 06/22. In the English half it states: “A lawyer will be assigned by Health and Human Services.” In the Spanish half it states: “Salud y Servicios Humanos te asignará un abogado.” The sentence does not identify **whom the lawyer represents**. The November 8 retrieved PDF text and page-image inspection both reproduced these statements. The source's crawl/retrieval metadata does not prove a server-side modification date.
2. **HHSC public FAQ:** https://www.hhs.texas.gov/contact/complaints-appeals/fair-fraud-hearings/fair-fraud-hearings-frequently-asked-questions . Its “Do I need to have an attorney?” answer says an appellant may have an attorney at their own expense and may contact legal aid about free help. This is **general HHSC appeals guidance**, not proof that every WIC-specific procedural deadline or entitlement is identical.
3. **HHSC Fair and Fraud Hearings Handbook:** https://fhb.hhs.texas.gov/handbooks/fair-fraud-hearings-handbook/1200-participant-rights-roles-responsibilities . Section 1231 recognizes the appellant's ability to present the case with a representative; section 1240 says an attorney representing an appellant must have appellant authorization. This further supports distinguishing agency counsel from the appellant's chosen representative.
4. **Previously documented program response:** [October 27 OT-003 publications referral](ot003-2026-10-27-agency-editor-referral.md) records a substantive HHSC Appeals Division clarification that an HHSC-assigned lawyer represents **the agency, not the appellant**, and that the publications editor had been contacted. The public record intentionally excludes private email metadata and personal details.

## Proposed replacement (DRAFT ONLY — subject to WIC publications/legal approval)

**English:** “The hearing itself is free. If Health and Human Services assigns a lawyer for the hearing, that lawyer represents the agency, not you. You may present your own case or choose someone to represent you, including a lawyer. A lawyer for you is not automatically provided by Health and Human Services. You may contact a legal aid organization to ask about possible free legal assistance.”

**Español:** “La audiencia en sí es gratuita. Si Salud y Servicios Humanos asigna a un abogado para la audiencia, ese abogado representa a la agencia, no a usted. Usted puede presentar su propio caso o elegir a alguien que le represente, incluso a un abogado. Salud y Servicios Humanos no le proporciona automáticamente un abogado para representarle. Puede consultar con una organización de asistencia legal sobre posibles servicios gratuitos.”

**Boundary:** Do **not** copy the general HHSC FAQ's 90-day appeal period into this WIC handout; the WIC handout separately states a **60-day** request period. Verify WIC-specific program rules before changing that deadline, the 21-day hearing timing, or the identity/role of counsel. The above draft is not official legal guidance and is not approved by the institution.
