## Distinct new evidence since the November 7 OT-007 review

1. **FSA Electronic Announcement GENERAL-26-42, originally June 29, updated October 30, 2026:** https://fsapartners.ed.gov/knowledge-center/library/electronic-announcements/2026-06-29/update-list-professional-degree-programs-due-court-order-updated-oct-30-2026 (alternate accessible print view: https://fsapartners.ed.gov/fsa-print/publication/1007281). The October 30 clarification adds Doctor of Naturopathy / Doctor of Naturopathic Medicine (N.D./N.M.D.), CIP 51.3303, and other programs in the same **four-digit CIP group that award the same credential**, for the duration of a District of Oregon preliminary injunction. The announcement also describes a District of Columbia preliminary stay and the interim program-by-CIP-and-degree list, including qualifying nursing, psychology and allied-health degrees. These are **temporary administrative designations subject to litigation**, not an unrestricted entitlement for every degree or every student in the discipline.
2. **Operational consequence in that same announcement:** During the court-order period, institutions may report qualifying programs at professional grade levels 10/11/13 and may correct previously originated 2026–27 graduate-level loans to reflect eligible higher professional limits; NSLDS can reevaluate aggregates. The Department explicitly warns institutions they may choose program-level limits and that later litigation could alter classifications. An institutional choice or student-specific exception must be checked; a listed program does **not** guarantee a $50,000 award.
3. **Current public article, fetched November 11:** https://studentaid.gov/articles/evaluating-financial-aid-offers/ . Its table still says "Direct Unsubsidized Loans | up to $20,500" and "Direct PLUS Loans | varies by student based on cost of attendance minus other financial aid received"; prose still says graduate/professional students can apply for PLUS. It also has a general warning that the Department is updating its pages. The Pell maximum is still $7,395 but the row is labeled 2024–25. This is a **high-level borrower-information gap**, not evidence that a school's actual award or eligibility engine is wrong.
4. **Official contemporary borrower-specific clarification:** https://studentaid.gov/plus-loan-credit-counseling states that new graduate/professional PLUS loans are generally unavailable after July 1, 2026 absent the limited pre-July-2026 program-borrowing exception; ordinary graduate Direct Unsubsidized annual cap is $20,500, qualifying professional cap $50,000, subject to additional limits. **Older baseline:** https://fsapartners.ed.gov/sites/default/files/2026-05/FrequentlyAskedQuestionsLoanLimits.pdf. The May FAQ alone is not sufficient to determine the **current court-affected list**.

## Precise scenario crosswalk (illustrative, not eligibility decisions)

| Scenario | What can be said safely |
|---|---|
| Ordinary new graduate borrower, no interim exception | Up to $20,500 annual Direct Unsubsidized, subject to program and individual eligibility; no new Grad PLUS. |
| Borrower in a **currently designated** professional-degree program, no interim exception | Potentially up to $50,000 annual Direct Unsubsidized; exact degree, CIP, aggregate caps, school limits, and current court-order status must be verified. |
| Qualifying N.D./N.M.D. program with matching CIP/credential | The Oct 30 FSA notice specifically includes it **during the preliminary injunction**; the designation may change. Not every naturopathy-adjacent program qualifies. |
| Qualifying pre-July-2026 continuing program borrower | An interim exception may preserve older loan rules, including Grad PLUS, subject to enrollment, prior borrowing and time/continuity conditions. |
| Parent of dependent undergraduate | Parent PLUS has separate 2026 annual and aggregate caps for non-exception borrowers; do not conflate with graduate PLUS or generic cost-of-attendance-minus-aid wording. |
