## What was actually verified

1. **Public aid-offer comparison article:** https://studentaid.gov/articles/evaluating-financial-aid-offers/ — retrieved on Nov 14 (reader crawl: previous day), lines 22, 46–62. Its general banner acknowledges federal program changes and ongoing updates. Nevertheless, the table still reads “Federal Pell Grant (2024–25)” with **$7,395**, “Direct Unsubsidized Loans” **up to $20,500**, and “Direct PLUS Loans” as cost of attendance minus other aid. The paragraph below still broadly says parents of dependent undergraduates and graduate/professional students can apply for PLUS. These are the same specific topics reported in the Sept 18 OT-007 complaint; this is a **due persistence check**, not a newly discovered institutional defect.
2. **Current Department loan-limit FAQ:** https://fsapartners.ed.gov/sites/default/files/2026-05/FrequentlyAskedQuestionsLoanLimits.pdf — May 20, 2026, pp. 1–3. New 2026–27 rules distinguish ordinary graduate from eligible professional borrowers and preserve **limited interim exceptions** for qualifying pre-July-2026 program borrowers. The exception generally depends on program enrollment by June 30, 2026 and a Direct Loan disbursed for that program before July 1, 2026, with additional duration and continuity conditions. The FAQ's particular scenarios control; a generic article must not determine individual eligibility.
3. **Official NSLDS processing announcement:** https://fsapartners.ed.gov/knowledge-center/library/electronic-announcements/2026-04-24/one-big-beautiful-bill-act-nslds-eligibility-processing-updates-updated-may-7-2026 — posted Apr 24, updated May 7, 2026; paragraphs “Graduate and Professional Aggregate Loan Limits” and “Parent PLUS Aggregate Loan Limit.” For borrowers **without** an exception, graduate/professional PLUS origination is no longer available starting July 1, 2026; Parent PLUS is subject to a **$65,000 aggregate cap per dependent student**, with exceptions. This announcement describes system implementation and itself cautions that additional rulemaking may affect technical guidance.
4. **Department's borrower-facing current guidance:** https://studentaid.gov/plus-loan-credit-counseling and https://studentaid.gov/exit-counseling/demo/prepare-to-repay/loan-forgiveness — official search-index content independently shows 2026 distinctions, including up to **$20,500/year ordinary graduate**, up to **$50,000/year eligible professional**, **$20,000/year and $65,000 aggregate Parent PLUS** for non-exception borrowers, and the limited Grad PLUS exception. **Limitation:** direct web-reader open of the first page returned no readable lines; the search-index copy was crawled approximately three months earlier. This source is corroborative, **not** proof of current rendered-page text. The directly opened official FAQ and NSLDS announcement remain the stronger evidence.
5. **Pell award-year context:** https://studentaid.gov/articles/dont-miss-out-on-pell-grants/ — retrieved Nov 14 (crawl two days earlier), explicitly identifies **$7,395** as the 2026–27 maximum. The aid-offer table's **year label** is stale; its Pell **dollar figure is not established as wrong**.

## Narrowly scoped replacement candidate — NOT SENT

For a qualified Federal Student Aid editorial/program reviewer, consider replacing the aid-offer table's generic loan entries and adjacent PLUS paragraph with an award-year note substantially as follows:

> **For 2026–27, federal loan limits depend on your borrower category, program, prior borrowing and eligibility for limited transitional exceptions.** Eligible graduate students generally have a $20,500 annual Direct Unsubsidized Loan limit; eligible professional students may have a $50,000 annual limit. For borrowers not covered by an applicable interim exception, new graduate/professional PLUS loans are unavailable beginning July 1, 2026. Parent PLUS loans for non-exception borrowers are subject to a $20,000 annual and $65,000 per-dependent-student aggregate limit. Qualifying continuing borrowers may be subject to different transitional rules. Confirm your individual eligibility with your school's financial aid office and current Federal Student Aid guidance.
