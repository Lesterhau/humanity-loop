## Fresh primary-source observations

1. **Public aid-offer article** — https://studentaid.gov/articles/evaluating-financial-aid-offers/ — read 2026-11-07. Its “Add Up Your Federal Student Aid” table still displays **“Federal Pell Grant (2024–25) | up to $7,395”**, **“Direct Unsubsidized Loans | up to $20,500”**, and **“Direct PLUS Loans | varies by student based on cost of attendance minus other financial aid received.”** Its following paragraph still states that parents of dependent undergraduates **and graduate/professional students** can apply for Direct PLUS. The page does carry a general warning that federal aid changes are being implemented and its information is being updated. These are direct current-page observations, not proof of an edit date.
2. **Official 2026–27 COD changes** — https://fsapartners.ed.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/CODSystemChanges2627.pdf (April 2026), pp. 2–4. For borrowers without an applicable interim exception, starting July 1, 2026: ordinary graduate Direct Unsubsidized annual limit **$20,500**; qualifying professional-program annual limit **$50,000**; new graduate/professional PLUS loans **eliminated**; Parent PLUS annual limit **$20,000 per dependent student**. Its system rules preserve exceptions for qualifying pre-July-2026 program borrowers. Therefore $20,500 is **still correct for ordinary graduate borrowers**, but the article's unqualified table omits the higher professional limit and new distinctions. Its generic PLUS description does not describe current eligibility or the parent annual cap.
3. **Official Federal Student Aid loan-limit FAQ** — https://fsapartners.ed.gov/sites/default/files/2026-05/FrequentlyAskedQuestionsLoanLimits.pdf (May 20, 2026), pp. 1–3, 20. Confirms the interim exception: qualifying borrowers enrolled in a program as of June 30, 2026 with a Direct Loan received for that program before July 1 can retain older loan rules for the defined expected-time-to-credential period, with conditions. Some grandfathered graduate/professional borrowers can continue Grad PLUS. It is inaccurate to say **all** graduate/professional PLUS loans ended without exception.
4. **Official FSA NSLDS announcement** — https://fsapartners.ed.gov/knowledge-center/library/electronic-announcements/2026-04-24/one-big-beautiful-bill-act-nslds-eligibility-processing-updates-updated-may-7-2026 (April 24; updated May 7, 2026). Explicitly says graduate/professional borrowers **not eligible for an exception** cannot receive Direct PLUS starting July 1, 2026, and establishes a new **$65,000 per-dependent-student Parent PLUS aggregate cap** for non-exception borrowers. The public article's cost-of-attendance-minus-other-aid description is not a complete current limit statement.
5. **Pell distinction already independently reviewed** — https://fsapartners.ed.gov/knowledge-center/library/dear-colleague-letters/2026-01-30/2026-27-federal-pell-grant-maximum-and-minimum-award-amounts (updated Feb 18, 2026). The **$7,395 Pell maximum remains correct** for 2026–27. The public article's **2024–25 year label** is stale; do not call its Pell dollar amount wrong. See existing OT-007 source review at `runtime/case-steward/ot007-2026-11-07-studentaid-aid-offer-year-label-source-review.md`.

## Finding / bounded recommendation

**Original report and current observations match.** The public aid-offer comparison article still lacks key 2026–27 borrower-category and interim-exception distinctions; this is a **substantive potential borrower-information clarity problem**, not merely an old Pell year label. It is not a finding that an individual aid offer or loan award was wrong, or that every student is eligible for $50,000.

**Human-review editorial proposal (NOT sent):** Replace the single $20,500 unsubsidized-loan maximum with an award-year-specific explanation differentiating graduate vs eligible professional borrowers; qualify graduate/professional PLUS eligibility with the July 2026 interim exception; separate Parent PLUS annual/aggregate limits from Grad PLUS rules; update the Pell row to 2026–27 while preserving the correct $7,395 figure; link to current FSA official loan-limit guidance. Keep the existing page's warning until the full update is reviewed by the responsible federal content team.
